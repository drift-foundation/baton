What the delivery suite actually drives
---------------------------------------

`_published_task` is where a task becomes a file: exclusive no-follow
creation, whole write, fsync, read-back comparison against the held bytes,
then 0444. `_proved_task` is the restart half — the same three questions asked
again of what is on disk. Those two functions ARE the delivery, so the cases
drive them rather than asserting around them:

  * two Jobs on ONE worker each publish their own `task.json`, read back and
    compared whole, with the `human_contract` artifact naming those bytes;
  * neither Job's bytes appear in the other's root, and the shared
    configuration is unchanged afterwards;
  * the two input identities differ, each equals its own composed manifest's,
    and neither is the installed one;
  * the implementation and review roles are delivered the SAME task for one
    Job — role instructions differ (projected out of `job_input_identity`)
    and the task does not, because a reviewer judging a different task than
    the implementer performed is the one thing a shared Job cannot survive;
  * restart: `_proved_task` accepts what was published and the file is 0444;
  * refused before launch: a restart that would deliver ANOTHER Job's task, a
    tampered file, a missing file, and publishing over an existing one;
  * preserved: a Job the reader answers `None` for, a worker with no reader,
    and a stage with no Job all receive the installed task;
  * `job_task_reader` over a real JobStore answers `None` for an unknown Job
    and for `None`, and the stored document verbatim for a row that has one.

What is NOT proved here
-----------------------

No provider, no container engine, no installed instance, no deployment, and no
connected lifecycle. The worker is composed the way
`test_single_worker.ThreeWorkersOneJobThreeImages` composes one — `__new__`
plus the held configuration — because `_for_job`, `_published_task` and
`_proved_task` read the held configuration and their operands and nothing
else; a case that needed a live runtime to ask a delivery question would be
measuring the fixture. So this claim proves the bytes that reach the input
root and the identity that accompanies them; it does not run a container over
that root.

Still owned by the PARENT W361912 and untouched here: the connected
implementation/review/correction/held rehearsal, the OBS-0 interval (no figure
offered; the timings above are harness wall time),
`NewJobBinding.create_binding` against an installed instance, and interruption
inside `bootstrap.prepare`.

No Git operation of any kind, in the repository or in the scratch roots.
