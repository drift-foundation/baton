What is proved here, and what is not
------------------------------------

PROVED in this claim, through the runtime's own entrypoints:

  * CRASH AND RESTART ADOPTION. The production composition dies inside the
    operation at six different checkpoints -- claimed, attempt, boundary,
    input, manifest, credential -- with no unwinding, and a fresh
    `operations_from` over fresh store handles resumes it. Each case asserts
    ONE launch and ONE claimed offer (the restart machinery's own questions)
    and then: the attempt is recorded against the delivered identity, the
    published root still carries the delivered task, the stage-less adapter
    over the adopted roots describes the delivered manifest, and
    `refresh_runtime` identifies the adopted runtime.
  * A RESTART THAT WOULD DELIVER ANOTHER RENDERING publishes nothing, starts
    nothing and changes no record -- refused by the identity comparison in
    `_matches` before any publication, which is EARLIER than the author
    expected. Asserted from the durable side and then asked of `_matches`
    directly.
  * A COMPLETE, INTERNALLY VALID SUBSTITUTED PAIR is refused by the durable
    record alone. Both `/input` documents are replaced by one consistent
    installed delivery minted for THIS attempt and THIS assignment;
    `check_input_pair` is called on the fixture inside the test and
    `read_input_root` accepts the root, so the refusal is provenance and not
    a malformed fixture. The earlier half-substitution case is KEPT and now
    describes itself correctly: it leaves a mismatched pair, which the pair
    rule refuses first.
  * ONE REQUESTED JOB THROUGH THE REAL LOOP, ended for real: the in-image
    program over the mounted exchange, then the Worker Manager's own ending
    through `sweep` to `completed`.

NOT PROVED, with the reason measured rather than assumed:

  * TWO JOBS THROUGH ONE LOOP. The bootstrap single-worker deployment cannot
    express it: `_matches` binds a stage's Work to this worker's manifest, so
    a second Job on another Work is never servable; and two Jobs over one
    Work do not finish it either, because the first ending passes the Work to
    its review route and the second stage's claim never completes (`offered`
    after 8 ticks in the case and 30 in a probe, with no attempt recorded and
    no roots created). The pool is where that behavior lives.
  * THE REVIEW-ROLE RUNTIME DELIVERY. `operations_from` calls `_held` with
    `roles=("implementation",)`, so a review configuration is refused
    outright; a review attempt mounts an upstream frozen checkpoint behind
    `review_driver.prepare_review`. The end-to-end harness for that is
    `ComposedOneJobCase`, whose setUp runs version-control init/add/commit in
    its fixture. This implementer's contract forbids mutating version-control
    operations anywhere, including a temporary fixture repository, so that
    harness was not run and no case was built on it. PROGRESS names the
    non-version-controlled route that remains open.

No provider, no container engine, no installed instance, no deployment. The
engine is tests.tools.test_single_worker.Engine (and its `quiescing` variant,
which models a stopped container stopping), one observable process boundary
without a daemon. The in-image worker is the real `baton_worker.serve_exchange`
with the scripted agent, which is how the owning suite drives an ending. The
stores are disposable SQLite files under temporary directories. No Git
operation of any kind, in the repository or in the scratch roots, and no test
fixture that performs one was executed.
