## The amendment, checked clause by clause against what the request named

**Exact outgoing-container termination before every normal handoff, maintenance
included.** TOK-5 now carries it as the v12 requirement and applies it to
"implementation, custody/maintenance, review and correction handoffs". The clause
that makes it real rather than aspirational is the one about natural exit: "A
naturally exited container qualifies only after the same exact positive
termination/exclusion checks" — a process that ended on its own is the case where a
manager is most tempted to skip the check, and it is named. HOST-1, ART-7 and
TOK-12 are consistently amended to *confirm the maintenance container and its
writers stopped* rather than merely to exclude a writer, so maintenance is no
longer a quieter path than production. ART-4 places review's immutable checkpoint
"after confirmed outgoing-container termination".

**Durable preservation with restoration by a new execution.** TOK-5 requires the
workspace/result and provider context saved to declared durable storage *before*
graceful shutdown, and forbids restarting the outgoing execution with writable
access — a later correction takes a new attempt, container and generation and
restores the retained state. CTX-1 separates the five lifetimes and states the
same order. CTX-3 has the property I would have asked for if it were missing:
"Stopping a container MUST NOT erase the sole reusable context or private
workspace", plus "A failed or incomplete final save must remain an explicit
recovery condition; do not claim successful reuse by silently starting a fresh
conversation." That closes the obvious failure mode of a shutdown-first rule —
losing the thing the next execution needs and reporting success anyway.

**Renewal is not handoff.** TOK-5: "Renewal within the same active assignment is
not handoff and does not require stopping after each tool call, database operation
or heartbeat." This is the clause that keeps the amendment from making ordinary
work impossible, and it is bounded to the same active assignment, so it cannot be
read as a general exemption. §19.2's renewal rule and TOK-9's mechanics are
untouched by the amendment, which is correct — renewal and handoff are different
events and the amendment only governs the latter.

**Expiry enforcement unchanged.** TOK-6, TOK-9's expiry/renewal competition and
§19.2 are as I signed them at baee80a3: expiry still begins revocation, still
requires the exact stop and positive cessation, and renewal must refuse once
expiry or revocation wins. The amendment adds a requirement to the *normal* path
without weakening the abnormal one, which is the right direction — the previous
text could be read as holding normal completion to a lower standard than expiry,
and that asymmetry is now gone.

**Live-mount handoff deferred with its history preserved.** CTX-1 and ART-4 both
state it: "V12 does not use live-container mount detach as the handoff proof",
"Keeping the live process across a mount detach/reattach is deferred", and §18
keeps the historical observations as what they were — "separately timed, not a
matched performance comparison" — with the deferral available as "a separately
selected change with measured benefit and complete isolation/recovery proof". A
deferral that records why the evidence does not yet support the optimization is
worth more than one that just postpones it.

**Section 17** adds the matching proof rows, and again they test the failure
direction: every normal role/resource handoff including maintenance waits for
confirmed termination and settlement, with done/output/detach explicitly
insufficient; and the context row requires restoration in a NEW container after
confirmed old-container shutdown with fresh execution/token identity. **Section
19.4** records the selection itself. **Section 18** keeps the boundary I rely on:
"A spec review signs the specification; it does not sign off implementation,
deployments or historical experiments."
