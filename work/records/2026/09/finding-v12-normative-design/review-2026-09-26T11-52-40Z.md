## Disposition

**NO WHOLE-DOCUMENT SIGNOFF**, and not only because section 19 is open — see
finding 1, which is a contradiction *among the settled requirements* that the
pending answer resolves in one direction or the other. Sections 1-18 and 20 are
otherwise coherent and I sign off on the settled requirements below with the
three required changes listed.

## 1. REQUIRED: TOK-7 and ART-7/ART-9 cannot both hold as written

**This is a concrete contradiction, not a deferral.** TOK-7 says: "until [the
placement decision] is resolved, this draft does not authorize host helpers as an
exception to Docker enforcement." But the settled artifact requirements place
real writers on the host:

* **ART-7** requires the *custody owner* to validate, freeze and retain the exact
  declared bytes and write its receipt "outside worker-writable storage" — a host
  write to managed storage, after container cessation, by construction not inside
  the token-bound container.
* **ART-9** requires deletion to hold "explicit custody/retention authority plus
  exclusive token ownership" — token ownership, yes, but the deleting writer
  itself is the host manager.

So a reader implementing ART-7 and ART-9 today implements exactly the host
writers TOK-7 declines to authorize. **Required change:** state in TOK-7 which
host-side writers the settled design already requires (custody freeze/receipt,
retention deletion, workspace allocation and reset), and say explicitly that
their *placement* is what section 19.1 decides — rather than leaving a blanket
non-authorization that the same document then contradicts. Either wording is
implementable; the ambiguity is not.

## 2. REQUIRED: section 19.1's two answers have different, unstated scope

19.1 asks whether *all* mutations "including deletion/reset" must run in
token-bound containers "or whether host helpers are permitted with separately
specified enforceable termination". The answers are not symmetric in cost and the
draft does not say so. If the answer is *containers for all mutations*, then
every host-side effect the design requires — ART-7's freeze and receipt, ART-9's
deletion, ART-4's workspace retention, HOST-1's pre-launch validation writes —
must be re-homed into a token-bound container, including the container that
performs the *reset after another container's cessation*, which TOK-6 sequences
after termination is proved. **Required change:** name that consequence in 19.1,
and state whether the reset executor is itself token-bound (a recursion the
current text leaves open).

## 3. REQUIRED: TOK-9's "host owns deadline measurement" needs its scope stated

TOK-1 makes ownership durable "across threads, processes and manager restarts",
and section 7 gives the inside-container manager real duties. TOK-9 then assigns
deadline measurement to "the host". **Required change:** say whether an
inside-container manager may ever hold a resource token, and if it may, which
clock decides its expiry. As written a conformant implementation cannot tell
whether TOK-9 is a division of labour or a prohibition.

## Settled requirements I confirm, with the checks behind each

* **DB-1/DB-2/DB-3** are exactly the rule W270664 was corrected against, at the
  right granularity: `stat` named explicitly, read transactions not exempt,
  callbacks and context-manager exits named, and "moving I/O outside a
  transaction without durable ownership is not a fix". I verified against the
  corrected boundary rather than from memory: the removal now admits in one short
  transaction, deletes with no lock held, and settles conditionally.
* **DB-6** matches the refusal accepted at 11:28:13Z, including its exact defeats:
  "Do not guess an alternate store from paths, open descriptors or `/proc`, or
  reopen a connection implicitly." Three rejected mechanisms of mine are
  enumerated in that dossier's PROGRESS; this clause is the correct rule and I
  confirm it is stated as behaviour rather than as my implementation's shape.
* **DB-7, TOK-5, TOK-11** correctly separate replay from repeated external effect
  and forbid a stale generation clearing a live one — the fencing defect the
  W270664 reviewer proved twice.
* **TOK-4** states reserve-before-launch with the property that matters: "A late
  launch cannot escape revocation because its start reply arrived after expiry."
* **TOK-6/TOK-8/TOK-10** keep cessation, settlement and acceptance as three facts
  and refuse a stop request as proof. TOK-8's example — a stale container cannot
  publish but can still damage a mounted workspace — is the precise reason a DB
  fence alone is insufficient.
* **Section 20** reads as a supersession map rather than a history: each row names
  the carried rule *and* the superseded interpretation, and the closing paragraph
  forbids relabelling old behaviour as compliance. Spot-checked against the
  token-baton ruling and the trusted-identity ruling; both rows match their
  sources' latest owner position.

## Disclosure: my own W270664 slice selected 19.1 by inference

Section 19 says "No implementation may select either unanswered policy by
inference". **The token slice I passed at W270664/274936 does exactly that**: it
binds the token to the container and stops it on expiry, while the governed
*effects* — the removal's deletion walk, intake's cleanup removal, allocation's
creation, custody normalization, and the token's own stop/confirm path — all run
host-side. That is the narrower reading of 19.1. I reported this to the owner in
poke 274913 and I repeat it here so the specification review is not the place it
goes unsaid: if 19.1 answers "containers for all mutations", that slice is bound
at the right identity with its effects on the wrong side of the fence, and those
five sites are the gap. W270664 is not accepted and is back with the owner.
