### R1 [P1] Serialize lifecycle admission before reading process records

Confirmed at v12/python/tools/stack.py:228–262: no lock covers ownership reads,
spawn and record publication, or coordinates start with stop. Two starts can
both read absence and both launch manager/publisher. A deterministic barrier at
the spawn boundary reproduced two successful starts, four live owned sleepers
(two of each role), and only two retained ownership records. The test serializes
individual record writes, so this result does not depend on a temporary-file
rename collision. The overwritten records leave processes outside later stop's
inventory. Sequential repeat-start coverage does not establish exclusivity.

Use a shared lifecycle lock or equivalent atomic admission covering the entire
ownership transition. Preserve positive process identity checks. Cover concurrent
start/start and start/stop using controlled schedules, including partial spawn
or record-write failure without orphaning an unrecorded process.

### R2 [P1] Unknown ownership must refuse replacement

Confirmed at stack.py:134–146 and :236–240: read_record distinguishes unreadable
from absent, but start feeds that result into alive(), clears it, and starts a
replacement. The reproduction retained a live original sleeper, supplied an
unreadable manager record, and observed successful replacement while the original
remained alive. Permission-denied process inspection is also collapsed into a
negative alive result by _stat; that is not positive proof of absence.

Keep absent, positively stale/reused, and unknown ownership distinct. Refuse
automatic start when ownership cannot be established; retain evidence and give
an actionable recovery reason. Cover malformed and unreadable records and
unavailable process inspection. Do not kill an unrelated process to recover.
