## [P0] The immutable entry account is still mutable on restart

**Observed.** Start from an ordinary activated target and queued entry, then
change only schema-valid persisted entry columns:

```sql
UPDATE entries
SET checkpoint_id = 'checkpoint-rewritten',
    candidate_digest = 'sha256:rewritten'
WHERE entry_id = 'entry-1';
```

`target_of` and `entries_of` accept the changed account. `grant_lease` then
leases it and returns the rewritten checkpoint and candidate digest. Because
the original checkpoint no longer appears in `entries`, `enqueue` also accepts
that same original checkpoint under `entry-2`.

This defeats the item-3 contract that entries and their Authority/checkpoint/
candidate evidence are immutable. In the item-4/5 consumer this is not merely
an audit discrepancy: selection can hand the integrator evidence different
from the accepted evidence that entered the queue, then admit the original
candidate a second time.

**Cause.** `_agrees_with_the_journal` never adopts `entry.enqueue`. It builds
operation identities from the rows currently present and checks only whether a
terminal decision name exists. Removing the duplicate eligibility document
made row/document disagreement unrepresentable, but did not make the row
immutable. The committed enqueue signature/result is the surviving original
account and is not compared.

## [P0] Deleted materialized history permits duplicate integration

**Observed.** Complete an ordinary enqueue, grant, integration, and release.
Delete the released lease and integrated entry, reset the target fence to zero,
and leave the committed operation journal intact. The resulting database
satisfies the owned SQL schema. `target_of` and `entries_of` accept it as an
empty target. A new entry id for the exact same Authority/checkpoint is then
accepted, receives fence 1 again, and is granted for integration.

The retained journal still contains the first enqueue, grant, integration, and
release, so this is a provable contradiction rather than unknowable loss. The
acknowledged deleted-row blind spot therefore violates deterministic queue
history, monotonic fencing, one-entry-per-candidate, and the acceptance rule

## Required correction boundary

The journal/materialized relationship must be proved in both directions at
every public read and before enqueue or selection:

- each committed target activation, entry enqueue, and lease grant relevant to
  a target has its one matching materialized row;
- every target, entry, and lease row has its exact committed originating act;
- the entry row matches the enqueue's target, entry id, allocated rank, and
  complete immutable eligibility account;
- the lease row matches the grant's target, entry, holder, attempt, and
  allocated fence; terminal acts and endings remain cross-bound as already
  required;
- the target fence is the greatest grant in durable history, not merely the
  greatest lease row that remains.

The journal record and its stored result/signature are persisted input and
must themselves be adopted under closed, typed semantics before serving as
evidence. A global operation scan is acceptable for this bounded item-3 slice;
an indexed/structured target binding is also acceptable if its schema and
adoption proof are complete.

The current implementation's in-action read is not a reason to weaken the
external invariant. A materialized transition and its operation record commit
atomically, so no external reader can legitimately observe only one. Either
make the proof aware of the exact pending act while inside `transact`, or stop
calling the fully journal-proved public projection between the materialized
write and journal insertion. The exception must be exact and internal, never a
general missing-journal allowance.

Add regressions for complete entry-account mutation, target/rank reassignment,
missing queued and terminal entry rows, missing live and ended lease rows,
fence reuse after deleted history, duplicate enqueue of the retained original
checkpoint, and malformed/mismatched originating operation evidence. Drive
each corruption through target, entry, lease, enqueue, and grant doors as
applicable and prove refusal mutates neither materialized rows nor the journal.
Keep positive first execution and exact replay cases, including the internal
transaction window.
