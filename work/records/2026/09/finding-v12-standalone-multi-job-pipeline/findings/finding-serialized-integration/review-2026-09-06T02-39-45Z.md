## Remaining findings

### P1 — the persisted blocked account is shaped but not cross-bound

`queue.target_of` decodes `blocked_account` through `_block_account`, but that
helper only validates member types. Its `canonical_target_id` parameter is
unused. The read does not prove that:

- `blocked_account.reason` equals `targets.blocked_reason`;
- its entry exists on this target, is `held`, and carries the same reason and
  detail in its settlement; or
- its lease exists on this target and agrees on entry, fence, integrator and
  attempt.

The write transition now constructs those relationships correctly, but a
restart trusts contradictory stored evidence instead of failing closed. The
re-review changed only the stored block entry from `entry-1` to the target's
queued `entry-2`; `target_of` returned it as accepted. Changing only the block
account reason likewise returned `blocked_reason='integrity'` beside
`blocked_account.reason='other-reason'`.

Correction boundary: `target_of` must adopt the entire blocked-target account
as one relationship. It must cross-check the target row, held entry and its
settlement, and the named lease/fence/holder account. The lease may be live or
properly abandoned, but its state-specific ending must itself validate.
Corruption regressions must cover each redundant member and refuse before
returning any target account.

### P1 — settlement and lease-ending variants are not semantically owned

`_adopt_entry` selects required settlement keys from the entry state, but does
not validate their member types. A held settlement with integer `reason` was
returned. `lease_of` requires only an `outcome` key for every ended lease and
treats `detail` and `recovery` as universally optional. An `abandoned` lease
whose ending was changed to `{'outcome': 'integrated'}` was returned as valid.

Correction boundary: define and own closed state-specific variants. At
minimum, refusal/held reasons are text, imported paths have their exact
collection/member shape, and lease endings distinguish integrated release,
entry-refusal release, and abandonment with its required recovery account.
Cross-check an abandonment recovery's attempt against the lease and the
blocked account. Add wrong-type, wrong-outcome, missing-recovery and
cross-attempt persisted-read cases.

### P1 — two mutation boundaries discard the owned snapshot

`settle_integrated` and `refuse_entry` call `boundaries.document(...)` but
discard the fresh owned document it returns. They then compute the signature
and persist the caller's original mutable dictionary. This violates the
boundary's explicit snapshot rule: the value validated must be the value used.

The re-review used the already-injected clock capability to mutate
`settlement.imported_paths` after validation/signature construction and before
the SQL arguments were assembled. The first call validated and signed
`['reviewed.py']`, persisted and returned `['other.py']`, and an exact retry of
the reviewed value replayed the other value:

```text
validated-a-stored-b {'imported_paths': ['other.py'], ...}
replay {'imported_paths': ['other.py'], ...}
```

Correction boundary: assign the return of `boundaries.document` and use only
that owned snapshot for signatures, persistence, results, and comparisons.
Audit every item-3 public document operand for the same pattern. Add a
deterministic mutation-at-clock/capability regression proving the journaled
signature and persisted/result account cannot diverge.
