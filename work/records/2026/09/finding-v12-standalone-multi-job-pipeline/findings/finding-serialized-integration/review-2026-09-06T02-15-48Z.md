## Findings

### P0 — an unresolved lease can be released and serialization advances

`queue.release_lease` checks that the lease is live, then ends it without
requiring the associated entry to have reached a terminal/reconciled state.
The entry remains `leased`. The next queued entry can therefore acquire a new
live lease while the prior entry still records unresolved canonical work.

The existing `AGrantIsProvedLiveWhereItIsUsed` test exercises this unsafe
transition by releasing a grant before settlement, but only asserts that the
old grant is no longer live. It consequently blesses the serialization hole.

Reproduction in `/tmp/w71878-item3-review.py` produced:

```text
premature-release ['leased', 'leased'] entry-2
```

Correction boundary: fuse normal release to the corresponding terminal
settlement/refusal, or make `release_lease` prove the exact entry has already
reached an allowed terminal/reconciled state and that the ending account is
the matching durable account. No public transition may end a live lease while
its entry remains `queued`, `leased`, `held`, or otherwise unresolved. Add a
negative regression proving premature release is rejected without changing
the lease, entry, target fence, or rank selected next.

### P0 — target blocking is not bound to the live lease and its entry

`queue.block_target` accepts an arbitrary queued or leased entry for the
target. It does not require or persist an association with the target's live
lease, fence, or holder account. `queue.abandon_lease` later checks only the
target state and the supplied live lease/fence; it can abandon the actual
holder while leaving that holder's entry `leased` and a different entry
`held`. Repeated block calls can similarly accumulate unrelated held entries.

Reproduction produced:

```text
cross-wired-block blocked ['leased', 'held'] abandoned
```

Correction boundary: make the durable blocked-target account identify the
exact entry, lease, fence, and recovery/holder account when a live grant
exists, require `block_target` and `abandon_lease` to cross-check that one
account, and define the accepted no-live-lease queued-block transition
separately if it is required. Add cross-entry, cross-lease, stale-fence,
repeated-block, and abandon-replay negative regressions. Each must leave the
original target, entry, and lease account unchanged.

### P1 — redundant persisted evidence is accepted when cross-wired

The entry row stores all 17 eligibility operands both as columns and in
`eligibility_json`, but `_entry` and `entries_of` parse the JSON without
proving that every member equals its column. `target_of` likewise does not
prove that the stored document's canonical target identity and digest agree
with the row, and lease reads do not prove that the lease target agrees with
its entry's target. These are receiving-boundary ownership checks, not merely
database conveniences.

After changing only the stored `proposal_id` column, the reader returned
contradictory evidence without refusal:

```text
split-evidence proposal-other proposal-1
```

Correction boundary: on every persisted read, own and validate the complete
row/document shape and all redundant identities, digests and relationships.
Fail closed before returning a target, entry, lease, or live grant. Cover each
redundant family with corruption cases, including the two digest families
whose values intentionally remain distinct but whose semantic operands must
cross-bind.

### P1 — adoption does not validate the safety schema

`IntegrationStore._adopt` validates the metadata kind/version and the
presence of four table names only. It accepts missing indexes, altered table
definitions, missing constraints, extra objects, and malformed metadata row
shape. In particular, dropping the partial unique index that is claimed as
the one-live-lease safety mechanism did not prevent reopening:

```text
missing-safety-index-adopted True
```

Correction boundary: validate the complete owned schema definition at the
storage boundary, including tables, columns, constraints, foreign keys,
indexes and their predicates, and reject unexpected owned objects or metadata
shape. Reuse the established v12 store-boundary pattern where applicable.
Add focused adoption-corruption tests, including removal or predicate change
of `leases_one_live_per_target`.

### P1 — Authority namespace accepts a malformed identity

`enqueue` validates `authority_uuid` as generic non-empty text. The store
accepted and returned `not-an-authority`, despite the contract describing
immutable Authority-namespaced entries:

```text
malformed-authority not-an-authority
```

Correction boundary: apply the repository's Authority UUID identity validator
at admission and persisted-read boundaries. This proves identity syntax only;
it must not be mistaken for Authority capability or policy eligibility.
