## [P1] A refused credential recovery stays claimed and is retried forever

`tools/single_worker.py:583-591` deliberately re-raises every recovery refusal
when the attempt has already reached `start-requested`. With no failed-start
record, `job_manager/manager.py:188-224` classifies that ordinary refusal as
`deferred`; the next level-triggered tick asks again. The new regressions make
the defect explicit rather than closing it:
`tests/tools/test_single_worker.py:731-733` and `778-781` run six more ticks and
assert that both the foreign-runtime and mismatched-mount stages remain
`claimed`.

That is not the accepted exceptional, non-retried failure ending. It also loses
the account the code says it preserves. In the exact mount-mismatch case the
first recovery stops the one identified runtime and performs bounded credential
cleanup; the next tick no longer finds the same lifecycle record, reconciles
absence, and later reports only a claimed stage. The original refusal is not a
durable status and is not in fact repeated unchanged.

Record the recovery failure through the already-requested start's owner ending,
preserving the recovery refusal and its bounded stop/cleanup evidence, so Job
status becomes `exceptional` and the manager stops asking. If an unresolved
foreign candidate must remain an operator condition rather than an ending,
represent that durably and non-runnably; an ordinary `claimed` stage polled on
every tick is neither an ending nor a safe wait state. Add assertions for the
second and later ticks, not only the first sweep report.

## [P1] Restart silently recreates a missing launch delivery

`tools/single_worker.py:611-625` always materializes a launch document when
`launch.adopt` answers absence, including when the attempt is already
`start-requested` or running. The launch owner's contract explicitly says
absence is ordinary only until a caller knows a runtime started; that caller
must refuse. The pinned W76207 finding likewise requires a complete launch
delivery to be adopted and contradictory or partial material to refuse rather
than be repaired.

This can place a new launch document under a container that may already hold
the mount, converting lost durable evidence into apparently valid state. Add
the required negative launch-replacement coverage and make materialization
reachable only from `not-started`; post-start absence must fail closed without
creating bytes or a replacement runtime.

## [P1] Launch validation happens after bearer recovery, with no safe unwind

The same ordering calls `_credential` at lines 615-616 before `launch.adopt` at
lines 618-625. On the attached-runtime path, `_credential` calls
`recover_credentials`, which proves the live container and then rereads and
registers the bearer bytes. If the launch root is malformed, `launch.adopt`
raises afterwards. The exception block at lines 628-635 intentionally does not
tear down an adopted delivery while its container may still hold the mount,
but it also retains no delivery capability and has no release-only operation;
the local object is dropped while its bearer registrations remain live. The
next tick can repeat the recovery and register them again.

Validate/adopt the post-start launch delivery before credential recovery, or
otherwise give the recovered delivery an explicit owned unwind that neither
removes a live mount source nor leaves unowned registrations. A regression
must corrupt the launch delivery in the published-credential restart window
and prove no bearer is reread/registered, no bytes are repaired, and subsequent
ticks do not repeat the act.

## [P1] The new preparation record contradicts the existing owner contract

`worker_manager/attempts.py:1330-1383` records a preparation that never reached
an adapter as `runtime.start-failed`, while deliberately journalling no
`runtime.start-requested` operation or lane. But the existing document contract
at `worker_manager/documents.py:149-163` defines `start_operation_id` as the act
the record followed, and the cleanup authorization at
`worker_manager/intake.py:2104-2128` defines this record as the manager's
account that a runtime came from a failed start. The new test even asserts that
the referenced start operation does not exist.

One durable record now has two contradictory meanings, and one meaning is a
destruction authorization. Either introduce an owner record for preparation
failure and let the Job projection unify the two outcomes, or explicitly
supersede the old contract and audit every reader/authorization against the
new meaning. Reusing the old kind only so the projection need not distinguish
them is not sufficient provenance for a durable act.
