## [P0] A proposal-specific call can integrate the queue's different head and
write the caller's Authority receipt

`admit_accepted` derives `entry_id`, `lease_id`, and the eventual Authority
receipt basis from its caller-selected `line_id`/`proposal_id`
(`driver.py:351-371`). It then calls `integrate_next` (`driver.py:428-433`).
That leaf deliberately grants the target's smallest queued rank, not the entry
the driver just admitted (`execution.py:293-308`, `queue.py:1594-1612`). With
two ready entries, the selected head can therefore be A while this call's
proposal is B. After A reports `integrated`, the driver calls
`_integrate_receipt(integrator, basis)` with B's proposal and checkpoint
(`driver.py:435-437`). The target bytes and coordinator settle A while the
Authority advances to B.

The restart branch has the same split: a lease derived during B's call may
hold A, but producer revalidation at `driver.py:417-420` checks B's separately
loaded entry/account rather than the granted assignment's A account. The
focused mock masks this defect by returning an arbitrary `entry-derived` from
`integrate_next` while asserting no equality with the locally derived entry
(`test_driver.py:293-310,332-336`).

Correct the ownership boundary so the exact coordinator-selected entry
determines the Authority, proposal, accepted evidence, sessions, and receipt,
or atomically restrict a grant to the exact admitted head without weakening
FIFO. Add a real two-entry regression in which B is submitted while A is
already ahead, and prove no A import can ever produce B's receipt.

## [P0] The live lease is released before Authority completion, and restart
automatically completes the forbidden missing receipt

The accepted parent contract requires the grant to be live immediately before
Authority completion and permits lease release only after that completion.
Instead, `settle_observed` records an integrated entry and immediately calls
`release_lease` before returning (`execution.py:422-429`). Only after that return does the driver
call Authority `integrate` (`driver.py:435-437`). The second target writer can
therefore acquire the newly open target while the first Authority target is
still stale. An Authority refusal or process crash in that window leaves the
filesystem/coordinator integrated and released with no Authority receipt.

On restart, `_terminal` sees the integrated entry and invokes
`_integrate_receipt` automatically (`driver.py:297-303`), even if no receipt
was ever written. That is specifically the completion action the 2026-09-06
manual-recovery ruling forbids after an interrupted cross-domain sequence.
The current regression codifies the unsafe behavior as expected
(`test_driver.py:368-377`) rather than proving an exact replay of a receipt
known to exist.

Keep the coordinator lease live through the Authority call and release it only
after the exact receipt is durably re-read. If interruption or refusal makes
completion uncertain, retain/block the target for explicit operator recovery;
do not synthesize the missing receipt on restart. This likely requires a
reviewed expansion of the execution/queue completion seam because the frozen
driver-only path cannot undo a lease already released by `settle_observed`.
