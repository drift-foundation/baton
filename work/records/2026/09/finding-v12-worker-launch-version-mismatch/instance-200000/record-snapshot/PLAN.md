## Why the correction is a RESUMED RECEIPT rather than a relaxed precondition

The review named two options and asked for research rather than a guess.

`intake_operation(attempt)` derives the `intake.record` identity from the
attempt row alone — no adapter bytes — so the committed receipt can be found
without collecting anything a second time. That makes "resume from the
committed receipt" available, and it is the option that preserves every
property the review requires:

* **no duplicate collection** — the adapter is not called at all on the replay
  path;
* **exact receipt/result identity** — the journal's own byte-stable result is
  returned, not a recomputed one;
* **custody unchanged** — `_collectable` and `_compared` still govern every
  genuinely new record, and no sealed output is admitted through them;
* **no false success** — only a `committed` journal state replays. A refused or
  absent record falls through to exactly the behaviour it has today.

Relaxing `_collectable` to admit `sealed` was the other option and is rejected:
it would let a second, DIFFERENT collection of an already-sealed attempt
proceed to the comparison, which is the opposite of taking custody once.
