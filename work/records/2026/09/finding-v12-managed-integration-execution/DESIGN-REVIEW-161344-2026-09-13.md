## Gate 1 — root/member capacity: ACCEPT, with two conditions

**Verified as stated in v2.** `stage_allocations` carries `FOREIGN KEY (stage_id,
episode) REFERENCES episodes(...)` and the configured-worker tuple FK, plus
`allocations_one_live_per_worker`, `allocations_one_live_per_principal` and
`allocations_one_live_per_stage_episode`. A second row for a child would indeed
fail those indexes, so "filter it in `reserve`" is correctly rejected.
`JobStore._schema_three_shape` derives the expected shape from `SCHEMA` plus
`MIGRATIONS` entries `>= 3`, so **additive** `CREATE` statements fit that
derivation and an `ALTER` of the old table does not — v2's reasoning holds.

**Verified: the release funnel is single.** `release` and `require_recovery` are
the only callers of `_move`, and `reconcile_allocations` reaches capacity through
exactly the branches v2 enumerates: an episode ending in `RELEASE_ENDINGS`,
`cleanup` in `complete`/`retained`, `_completed_integration`, and
`launch-failed-before-runtime`, with `require_recovery` for failed cleanup or an
uncertain runtime. Guarding every root release at `_move` is therefore
implementable at one point rather than scattered.

**Condition 1 — the guard must not block quarantine.** `require_recovery` also
goes through `_move`. A root that cannot be moved to `recovery-required` because
its memberships are not ended would be worse than the defect being fixed: the
uncertain case is exactly when capacity must stay held *and* visible. State
explicitly that the guard applies to the `released` transition only.

**Condition 2 — the success ordering is load-bearing against a named branch.**
On a successful apply the branch that fires is `_completed_integration`, which
releases on validated completion and is deliberately decided **before** the
quarantine branch. v2's "close admission before committing the final root ending"
must therefore be tested against that exact branch, not in the abstract: if the
root is still `open` when a validated integration completion is observed, the
guard has to refuse a release that today happens unconditionally.

Nothing else in gate 1 is contradicted by what I read. The membership constraints
(one root per allocation, at most one `admitted` or `recovery-required` member,
apply naming the parent's actual attempt) are new invariants, not existing
behaviour, and the design says so.

## Gate 2 — fenced-before-start: REFUSED as scoped, and the exact reason

This is the one gate I cannot affirm, and v2's own escape clause is the remedy.

**What I read.** `request_runtime_start` refuses on exactly two attempt facts:
`assignment_generation is None` ("not activated"), and
`attempt["execution_runtime"] != "not-started"` ("already-terminal"). It then
checks the lane predecessor, asks the adapter for its plan, and journals the
start. **Nothing in that path consults the cancellation axis.**

`request_cancellation` journals its intent, fences the generation at the
Authority through `port.cancel`, records session quiescence, and then orders
quiescence. Cancellation lands on the `worker_disposition` axis — whose table is
`"none": [... "cancelled"]` — and, with no runtime attached, `_order_quiescence`
reports `ordered=false` and **nothing on the `execution_runtime` axis moves**.
The attempt keeps `execution_runtime = "not-started"` and keeps its
`assignment_generation`.

**Therefore:** after cancelling an unstarted attempt, a subsequent
`request_runtime_start` is not refused by the preconditions in that function.
A reader over these owners can honestly report *"no start had been committed as
of this read"*; it **cannot** report *"a cancelled execution axis prohibits a
subsequent start"*, because no such axis rule exists. The proposed
`unstarted_cancellation_of` would be attesting a property its owners do not
enforce, and releasing scheduler capacity on it would be the fabricated
quiescence the owner outcome forbids.

**Bounded correction, which v2 already anticipates.** Its own sentence — *"No
generic attempt start/cancel transition rewrite is proposed; return the exact new
scope if the no-start proof requires one"* — is triggered. The minimum is a
durable no-start latch that the start transaction itself honours: either
`request_runtime_start` additionally refuses a committed cancellation for the
fixed assignment, or cancellation of an unattached attempt commits a terminal
`execution_runtime` value that the existing "already-terminal" check catches. The
first is narrower. Whichever is chosen, it is a change to the start transition and
belongs in the returned scope **before** the reader is accepted, not after.

Until then: keep the capacity held. An unstarted, fenced apply attempt with a
failed preparation is an operator-visible hold, which is worse ergonomics and
better truth than a release justified by a reader that cannot see a race it does
not gate. Both race orders must still be tested when the latch exists — I agree
with v2 that "if the start committed, hold and reconcile" is the other half.

## Gate 3 — portable custody and 5→6: ACCEPT, with one correction

**Verified.** `integration/schema.py` is `SCHEMA_VERSION = 5` with
`MIGRATIONS = {}` — there is no migration path today, so an explicit bounded
5→6 is genuinely new scope rather than a reuse. `integration_results` declares
`workspace TEXT NOT NULL`, `target_source TEXT NOT NULL` and
`target_reference TEXT NOT NULL`: the path-bearing columns are **required**, so a
portable row cannot reuse that table without dummy values. v2's refusal to write
dummies is correct.

**Correction — the one-result key is a NEW invariant, not a preserved one.** v2
says per-table uniqueness alone would allow two results for one key. True. But
the existing table has **no** uniqueness on
`(canonical_target_id, source_proposal_id, target_revision)` at all: its only
unique constructs are the `result_id` primary key and
`leases_one_live_per_target`. So the upgrade must not assume legacy history
satisfies the key. Two consequences the design should state:

1. adding such an index to the legacy table during 5→6 can **refuse to build**
   on real data, which would turn an additive migration into an unopenable
   store; and
2. the cross-representation check therefore belongs in the writer (as v2 says),
   and the upgrade must *measure* whether legacy rows already violate the key and
   say what it does then — refuse the upgrade with the offending ids, or record
   them as pre-existing history that the new writer will not extend.

With that stated, the storage choice is sound: a separate
`managed_integration_results` with content-addressed artifacts, old readers
unchanged, read-only open recognising 5 and 6, and no second writable copy of a
live target.

**Also verified as a real consumer:** `StageDeployment.judge_result` clones the
judgment snapshot from `result["workspace"]["path"]`. v2 is right that this must
obtain the candidate from the collected portable artifact in the managed case;
it is the concrete reason portable custody cannot be deferred.

## Gate 4 — trusted target-owner entrypoint: ACCEPT

The operand list of `publish_collected_integration(canonical_target, entry,
lease, fence, admitted_old_revision, authorized_candidate,
collected_phase_reference)` matches what the existing finalization actually
needs, and keeping the compare-and-swap against the **admitted** old revision
rather than the current reference preserves the existing rule that drift is a
refusal rather than a baseline. Resolving `target_root`/`source` locally on the
executor node is the right shape, and it is the adapter gap my previous review
named.

**One condition:** "proves exact stopped execution" must name whose evidence it
is. The only acceptable source is the trusted runtime adapter on the node that
ran the execution; a worker phase report and a coordinator-side runtime identity
from A are both inadmissible here. Say so in the interface contract, not only in
the prose around it.

## Gate 5 — slice 1 scope and the verification envelope: ACCEPT, with a sequencing note

The slice-1 path list is finite and each entry is justified by a gap I verified:
the allocation FKs and unique indexes, the required path-bearing result columns,
the absent integration migration, and the missing target capability signature.

**Sequencing note:** slice 1 as listed includes the no-start reader
(`worker_manager/attempts.py`, `worker_manager/documents.py`). Under gate 2 that
item cannot be finished as scoped. Either drop it from slice 1 and carry the
capacity hold until the start-transition scope is approved, or bring the returned
start-transition scope into slice 1 with it. Do not implement the reader against
the current start rule.

The proposed **120 s author / 30 s reviewer** envelope is proportionate for
focused owner tests over new tables and readers, given this Work starts at zero.
The persisted per-command discipline it describes — fresh-sum the ledger, record
cap, spent, remaining, expected, margin and timeout beside each run, refuse when
cost plus margin cannot fit, charge failures and timeouts — is the same discipline
an owner ruling already made mandatory elsewhere and it has since been exercised
in practice; inheriting it here is right. It is a proposal, and I am not granting
it.

## Disposition

**Gates 1, 3, 4 and 5 accepted** with the conditions and the one correction
above. **Gate 2 refused as scoped**, with the exact failed invariant named: the
start transition does not consult the cancellation axis, so no reader over these
owners can prove no-future-start, and capacity must stay held until a durable
no-start latch is approved and implemented.

This is a design acceptance boundary for owner scope approval, not implementation
authority, and I have not implemented any of it. Predecessor spending is

unknown activities and reviewer 147.7342205499972 s; W103525 retains its own
ledgers; W161230 measured verification remains zero.
