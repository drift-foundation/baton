## The three doors, each refusing for its own reason

Reproduced in `test_abandonment.py`,
`test_a_cancelled_receiptless_attempt_has_no_door_to_cleanup`: a real
correlated faulted attempt, cancelled, then reconciled to rest — `quiescent`,
`cleanup: pending`, no intake receipt, a pin whose policy is `None`. Every
supported ending is then tried.

**Door one — `intake.abandon_attempt`** (`intake.py:2321`). Refuses at the
Authority: `assignment generation was fenced and ended`
(`authority/core.py:1297`, `_expect`). The abandonment derives its OWN fence
identity — `_abandon_fence_operation_id` (`intake.py:3241`), used by
`_abandon_intent` (3256) and spent at the `port.cancel(...)` fence (≈2447) —
so over a generation the cancellation already fenced it issues a SECOND cancel
rather than replaying the first.

**Door two — `intake.authorize_cleanup`** (`intake.py:1120`). Does not refuse.
With no intake receipt it answers `_block_on_intake` (`intake.py:4059`), moves
the cleanup axis to `blocked-on-intake`, calls no adapter and commits no
cleanup record. The door opens onto a room with no exit.

**Door three — `deadlines.advance_deadline`** (`deadlines.py:348`). This is
the one place this build DOES recover a fenced generation, and it does it
correctly: `_cancel_for_deadline` (≈330) retries the ALREADY-ISSUED authority
operation taken from the committed cancellation intent —
`port.cancel(assignment, intent["authority_operation_id"], reason, …)` —
and adopts it through `intake._abandoned_fence`. It then runs
`intake._authorize_deadline_cleanup` (`intake.py:3755`) and
`discharge_deadline_quiescence_gate`. It refuses here because the pin carries
no policy.

Result: the runtime, its two roots and the installed
`runtime-quiescence:<generation>` gate stay unsettled, with no operation that
can settle them.

## The proposal — and it is not mine to apply

`src/baton_v12/worker_manager/intake.py` is **not an owned path**, so this is
an exact proposal for coordination rather than a change.

**Correction A (preferred).** In the abandonment's fence step, when a committed
cancellation intent already exists for this attempt, REPLAY that Authority
operation id and reason instead of deriving a second fence identity, and adopt
the answer through `_abandoned_fence` — which is precisely what
`deadlines._cancel_for_deadline` already does and is already accepted. Exact
sites: `intake.py:3241` `_abandon_fence_operation_id`, `intake.py:3256`
`_abandon_intent`, and the `port.cancel` at ≈2447. The reader is the same one
the deadline owner uses (`deadlines._cancel_intent`).

Everything downstream is already receiptless-safe and needs no change:
`_destroyed_abandoned` (3469), `_normalized` (3709),
`_settle_recordless_cleanup` (3818), `abandonment_cleanup_of` (2791) and
`discharge_abandoned_quiescence_gate` (3081).

**Correction B (smaller blast radius, worse fit).** Give
`_authorize_deadline_cleanup` an entry for a policy-null pin. Rejected on
reading: it overloads the deadline vocabulary for a stop that has nothing to do
with a deadline, and the receipts would be recorded under
`runtime.destroy-deadline` for an attempt no deadline ended.

**Not proposed, and deliberately so.** Catching the Authority refusal and
answering success; removing or relaxing the fence check; minting a replacement
assignment; or weakening the duplicate-effect coverage. Each would turn an
unsettled runtime into a manager that says it settled one.

## What this does NOT claim

Correction A is a FORWARD fix. Applying it would make the preserved run's two
attempts recoverable — they satisfy its precondition exactly, since both
carry a committed `attempt.cancel` with its authority operation id — but
that is a consequence to be proved after the correction exists, not a
claim made now.

A separate forward-looking option lives in an owned path:
`tools/single_worker.py` could pass a `deadline_policy` at
`request_runtime_start`, which would put future attempts inside the deadline
owner's accepted recovery path. It changes start semantics for every attempt
this composition makes and **does not recover the preserved run**, so it is
recorded as an option for the owner rather than taken.
