## [P0] Capability decisions use deployment scope instead of the target Work

`Core._require_capability` calls `authorize(actor, capability=...)` without a
scope (`core.py:1415`). Receipt and close call sites therefore resolve in
`scope:deployment`, even when the proposal or Work belongs to another
authority-owned scope. `close` does the capability check before loading its
Work (`core.py:1664`), and `_write_receipt` does it before loading the proposal.

The public-surface reproduction retained at `/tmp/w16821_review_repro.py`
creates a `scope:platform` Work and proposal:

- a verifier granted `verify` only in `scope:platform` is refused;
- granting the same verifier in `scope:deployment` succeeds and the receipt
  falsely records `effective_scope = scope:deployment`;
- a closer granted `close` only in `scope:platform` is refused, while the
  deployment-scoped grant closes that Work.

This is the scope-widening failure the seam exists to prevent. Derive the
effective scope from the exact target Work for close and from the proposal's
exact Work for every workflow receipt/integration attempt. A caller-supplied
scope remains forbidden. Add positive cross-scope and negative
deployment-grant-does-not-authorize tests for each capability-bearing door.

## [P0] Close and attributable acts do not expose the decision they used

The acceptance explicitly requires claim, close, and workflow-actor evidence
to expose endpoint separately from principal, effective scope, role,
grant/mask provenance, and policy generation. The implementation itself admits
that `activity`, `contract_event`, `proposal`, and `integration_attempt` carry
none of that, and close persists no decision at all. The reproduction confirms
an unclaimed close leaves neither Work decision columns nor an assignment
event. A stale-target integration attempt likewise journals an attributable
authorized actor without its authorization decision.

Directly authorized acts (claim, close, each receipt, and the durable refused
integration attempt) must retain the exact decision taken for that act.
Assignment-derived acts may copy the immutable claim decision or durably join
to it through the full exact assignment identity, but the public projection
must expose the same complete typed decision and preserve it after release,
restart, reconfiguration, and close. Do not infer current mapping or policy
when reading history.

## [P1] The public claim projection hides the fields the row persists

`assignment_event` stores principal, effective scope, grant provenance, and
policy generation, but `assignment_events` (`core.py:628`) discards all four.
The new test reaches into raw SQL to assert them, so it does not establish the
acceptance's public evidence boundary. Project the immutable decision beside
the exact assignment reference and test only the public projection for this
contract.

## [P1] Capability projection collapses scope and provenance

`capabilities_of` (`core.py:416`) selects only capability names. With one
principal granted `verify` in deployment and platform scopes, the public answer
is `['verify', 'verify']`: it neither identifies the effective scopes nor the
direct provenance and is no longer a stable capability projection. Deliberately
version or replace this projection with scope- and provenance-bearing entries;
retain a compatibility helper only if its duplicate/flattening semantics are
explicit and useful.
