## [P0] A later v11 claim rewrites the apparent decision of earlier activity

`Core._claim_decision_for` finds a claim by `(work_id, participant,
generation)` and chooses the newest matching event (`core.py:386-396`). That
tuple distinguishes v12 claims because they mint generations. A v11 assignment
has a null generation, so release and reclaim by the same endpoint produce two
distinct claim acts with the same join fields.

The public reproduction in
`evidence/w16821-v11-reclaim-history-repro.py` writes activity under a first v11
claim, releases it, rebinds the endpoint to another principal, claims again and
writes a second activity. `assignment_events` correctly retains the two claim
principals as `principal:baton.claude` and `principal:one-person`; after the
second claim, `activities` projects both activities as authorized by
`principal:one-person`. The first activity's historical principal and policy
generation therefore change without the act or decision row being rewritten.

This violates the recorded requirement that every assignment-derived act
durably join to the exact immutable claim decision and that public history not
be re-derived. Persist an exact claim-event reference on assignment-derived
rows (or an equivalent immutable act identity) and use it for projection. The
nullable assignment tuple, timestamps and newest-row ordering are not exact
claim identities.

Add a v11 release/rebind/reclaim/reopen regression covering both claim events
and both activities, plus a removal mutation for the durable binding. Retain
the existing v12 historical case.
