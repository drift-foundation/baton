## Findings

1. **[operator/approver] The credential source needs checking before another
   attempt.** Every other input is now proven good: task, delivery, image,
   network, posture, retention, arc. An unchanged retry under this Work's
   standing authorization would very likely reproduce this exact result, so it
   is not worth spending until the source is confirmed.

2. **[P1, `claude_agent`] A provider failure is not diagnosable from the
   retained evidence.** The account says `status: 1` and, by a design whose
   reasoning is sound, nothing else. It took three probes outside the attempt
   to reach "probably the credential", and a supervised pilot cannot depend on
   an operator thinking of them. The CLI already emits a structured,
   non-prose `terminal_reason` on stdout rather than in the stream that may
   carry the bearer. The safe form is a CLOSED VOCABULARY — map it to one of
   this deployment's own fixed words and publish only the mapped word, never
   the provider's text. W17110's `trial.mjs` classifier, which the frozen task
   exists to add coverage for, is that shape of work already. `claude_agent`
   is not this child's to change; this is offered rather than taken.

3. **[P2, operator] The `now` grant is judged against a 120-second offer TTL,
   after the source is staged.** My first invocation of this attempt set `now`
   about twenty minutes ahead of the deployment clock. `issue_offer` computes
   the expiry from the manager's own clock and `accept_offer` compares the
   grant, so it refused `offer ... expired` after `stage_source` had written
   the delivery and an offer had been issued. The manager settled the offer
   `expired` cleanly and no claim, runtime or provider turn followed — but
   `preflight` cannot catch this and nothing warns that `now` is clock-relative
   rather than a free identity. Recorded with fresh identities and a real
   instant used for the attempt that ran.
