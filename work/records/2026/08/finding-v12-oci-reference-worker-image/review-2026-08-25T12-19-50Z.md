## [P1] Independent builds deliberately have different immutable image identities

The confirmed acceptance requires a reproducible image recipe with an
immutable base/**image digest**. The downstream manager accepts and launches
that image digest. After disabling cache, however, the correction records two
different image IDs and adds
`test_the_image_id_is_a_receipt_and_not_the_artefact`, which requires them to
remain different. It then calls equal `RootFS` layer digests plus five selected
configuration members “the artefact.”

That is a weaker, contradictory contract. The OCI image identity is the
content digest of its config object; equal filesystem layers do not make two
different config objects one image, and comparing a selected subset of config
does not prove equality of the omitted image metadata. Most importantly, it
does not produce the one immutable image digest that the acceptance and the
manager say will be pinned.

The additive daemon-backed
`test_two_independent_builds_have_one_pinnable_image_identity` states the
existing acceptance at the consumer boundary by comparing the two independently
built image IDs. The implementer's own recorded measurement establishes that
it fails on Docker 29.1.3; it also directly contradicts the new test that
requires inequality. That contradiction must remain visible rather than being
resolved by silently weakening “image digest” to “layer set.”

Use a deterministic image-output path that normalizes or omits volatile config
metadata and produces one OCI image identity for the pinned platform. If the
available deployment intentionally cannot do that, obtain and record an
explicit approver supersession of the confirmed acceptance; until then the
required gate must fail as unsupported rather than certify a different
property. Do not remove or weaken the additive identity regression without
that ruling.
