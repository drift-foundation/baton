## D1 — due is level-triggered actionable state, not a delayed audit act

**Recommend:** derive due state from `review_at` and the current time and expose
it continuously in the responsible route's actionable projection. A blocking
`wait` surface may calculate the nearest deadline and return when it becomes
due without mutating authority. No durable `due` audit row is needed: reaching
time is not an actor decision, and emitting such a row only on a later
unrelated write is not notification. Extension, abandonment, resume, and close
remain explicit audited acts. The same due item remains visible across reads
and restart until one of those decisions changes it.

This keeps reads pure, needs no daemon, wakes a live waiter at the right time,
and cannot duplicate a one-shot event because due is level-triggered state.

## D2 — verification assignment is a specialized exact `@` obligation

**Recommend:** retain one common obligation identity/addressability/actionable
projection, with a `verification` subtype and one-to-one structured assignment
detail for candidate, round, observation, evidence, assessment, and withdrawal.
Use the specialized `report` operation instead of an ordinary free-form
response. Verification reports never wake or transition provider Work; the
specialized subtype is ineligible as a WS-1 obligation-backed waiting
condition. It remains an exact `@` obligation in the collaboration vocabulary
and becomes non-actionable by report or withdrawal.

This preserves the user's ruled meaning—another route is required to act while
Current remains with the provider—without forcing structured verification
state into the classic response payload or accidentally making feedback vote.

## D3 — required dependencies target open Work only

**Recommend:** refuse creation of a new `blocked_by` edge whose provider Work
is already terminal. A closed provider cannot block future execution. Link it
as historical context or create/link follow-up Work and block on that open
record. Existing dependency edges remain historical and receive the provider's
explicit terminal outcome when it closes.

This prevents a newly added gate from immediately clearing against a provider
that may lack the context/outcome the consumer meant to rely on.

## D4 — every terminal close records its outcome

**Recommend:** require exactly `satisfying` or `non-satisfying` for every Work
close, not conditionally based on the graph shape observed at commit. The
outcome states whether the Work's terminal conclusion met what it was supposed
to deliver. It is useful for parents, dependents, later follow-ups, and the
Work's own audit even when no consumer edge currently exists. Closure still
ends every gate either way; downstream handlers decide what that evidence
means.

This removes graph-history conditionals and prevents Work closed before a
consumer links or drills from having an undefined terminal meaning.

## Closed-Work wording clarification

No separate product choice is needed if D3 is accepted. Historical reads,
messages, audit records, links, search, and traversal continue to reference
closed Work. Workflow mutation of the closed record is refused. New Work may
name the closed record through non-gating `follow_up_of`; required gating must
target the new open Work.
