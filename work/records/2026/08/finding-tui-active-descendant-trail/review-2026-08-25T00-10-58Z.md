The additive projection-minor boundary, hidden-claim selection, ordinary-row
deduplication and same-snapshot placement are directionally correct. Three

## [P1] A deep filter match loses the structural context the ruling preserves

The approved clarification says that when a hidden active Work matches the
canonical filter, its bounded-window ancestors remain as structural context,
just as they do for an ordinarily visible matching descendant. The
implementation instead leaves `rows=[]` and makes `active_trails[0].anchor`
the requested root even though that root is absent from `rows`. The future
renderer therefore has no visible ancestor under which to group or insert the
trail, and the returned anchor is not the “nearest returned ancestor” the
approved shape names.

Retain the root/child/grandchild bounded context with
`filter_match: false`, anchor the trail to the deepest retained row and keep the
hidden matching Work in `active_trails`. The existing
`test_a_handler_filter_finds_the_claim_the_window_hides` assertion that rows
stay empty enshrines the opposite of the confirmed ruling and must be corrected
under that case-specific approval. The additive
`test_a_deep_filter_match_retains_its_visible_ancestors` reproduces the defect.

## [P1] Trail Work rows discard their canonical claim facts

`_active_trails` calls `_row_view` with `claimed_ats={}` and `handoffs={}`.
For every actively claimed endpoint, `_row_view` therefore publishes
`claimed_at=None` and `heartbeat_at=None` even while `detail` for the same Work
publishes the current claim event timestamps. A trail is the exact active Work
row, not a reduced row whose Handler survives while its claim facts disappear.

Batch `_claimed_ats` and `_handoffs` across all trail endpoints, reuse one
sampled `now` for their row/runtime derivation and preserve parity with the
canonical row facts. The additive
`test_a_trail_row_keeps_the_claim_facts_of_the_canonical_row` reproduces this.

## [P1] Multiple trails use endpoint order, not full containment order

The approved order is the full canonical containment order. The implementation
sorts endpoints using the global `WORK_ORDER` position of each claimed Work.
Two claims beneath different hidden branches of one visible anchor can
therefore leapfrog their branch order when the endpoint priorities differ.

Order by the complete root-to-endpoint sibling-order path, grouping by the
nearest visible anchor. `fixtures.build_crew` already supplies the two eligible
handlers the handoff said were unavailable. The additive
`test_same_anchor_trails_follow_full_containment_order` proves the reversed
result without weakening the one-claim-per-participant invariant.
