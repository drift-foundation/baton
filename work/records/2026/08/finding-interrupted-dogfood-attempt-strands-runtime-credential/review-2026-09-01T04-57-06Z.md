## Findings

### [P1] The earliest interruption still leaves the bearer with no recovery path

`_launched` materializes the credential before `run_dogfood_task` records or
activates the attempt. When the process dies in that window,
`attempt_runtime_of` returns `None`; `_pre_attach_recovered` then calls
`label_context`, which refuses because no activated attempt exists, and the
command returns an unresolved record without removing anything
(`v12/python/tools/dogfood_operator.py`, `_launched`, `recover_abandoned`, and
`_pre_attach_recovered`).

This is not merely an uncovered edge. The new regression
`test_an_interruption_before_attach_invents_no_terminal_attempt` passes by
asserting that the real credential root is still present after `--abandon`.
That reproduces the finding's sharpest failure: a supervising process can die
after materialization and leave a readable bearer with nothing able to prove
and end it.

**Required correction.** Ensure credential material cannot exist without a
durable manager-owned recovery selector. Reorder materialization behind a
durable attempt/label context or commit an equivalent bounded recovery
identity before materialization, then make `--abandon` prove runtime absence
and clean the exact orphan without inventing a terminal attempt. Do not treat
raw grants or a missing selector alone as authority to delete.

### [P1] An orphan capability can tear down a different attempt's credential

`OrphanTeardown` records its own `attempt_id`, but `OciAdapter` checks only its
nominal type. `_torn_down` receives runtime absence without the cleanup
command's attempt identity and invokes the orphan unconditionally. Nothing
binds the credential target to `runtime_attempt_id` before the destructive
act (`v12/python/src/baton_v12/worker_manager/oci.py`, `OciAdapter.__init__`,
`destroy_abandoned`, `_removed`, and `_torn_down`).

**Observed reproduction.** `/tmp/w55758-review-probe.py` constructed an
adapter over assignment roots for `attempt-1` with an `OrphanTeardown` for
`attempt-other`. Positive runtime absence returned `torn-down` and removed
`attempt-other`'s real material. The probe printed `other_present_before:
true` and `other_present_after: false`.

**Required correction.** Compare the orphan attempt with the exact cleanup
command's `runtime_attempt_id` before engine removal or credential teardown.
A mismatch refuses with no engine act and leaves both attempts untouched.
Cover the destructive negative case through the public cleanup seam, not only
by calling `_torn_down` with a correctly paired fixture.

### [P1] A fault after cleanup starts can leave no recovery record

`_abandoned` writes the recovery document only after `recover_abandoned`
returns. The pre-attach path performs `orphan.tear_down()` and launch cleanup
without converting teardown failures into the record's unresolved account.
A real multi-home ending can remove the first home and then refuse on the
second, after an external mutation but before any durable recovery document
is written.

**Observed reproduction.** `/tmp/w55758-review-probe.py` injected a failure
after marking an external act. `_abandoned` closed its capabilities and
propagated `RuntimeError`, but `recovery_record_written` was false. This is
also contrary to `main`'s existing contract that a post-start fault propagates
only after the attempt record is written.

**Required correction.** Once recovery mutation begins, preserve a bounded,
closed recovery account even when a later credential home, launch ending, or
composition step fails. Expected cleanup refusals become named `unresolved`
facts; unexpected faults still propagate only after a safe adapter-authored
record is attempted, without copying raw exception text. Add a real
two-home partial-failure case and prove an exact retry completes rather than
replaying already-settled acts incorrectly.
