2. THE DEFECT

`drain_dispatch` sampled `store.clock()` inside its mutation for the
`drain_requested` row and the singleton's draining transition. `_write` then
called `self.clock()` AGAIN and passed that second reading into
`_settle_dispatch` for `pause_reached` and the paused transition.

Both readings come from the same clock. That is what my round-2 regression
asserted, and with `BATON_WORK_NOW` fixed to one constant it could not tell
"same source" from "same instant" — every sample is identical when the clock
never moves. The property that actually matters is the second one: an empty
drain writes both control events at ONE authority sequence because they are
one indivisible committed act, and an act has one instant.


3. THE CORRECTION — ONE SAMPLE PER WRITE

`Authority.instant()` is the sampled instant of the write in progress.
`_write` takes it ONCE, inside the transaction and after the operation-replay
check (a replay performs nothing and has no instant of its own), and clears it
in a `finally` however the write ends. Every writer that needs the act's time
reads it back:

  authority.py   _write:            self._instant = self.clock()   (once)
                 _settle_dispatch(self.conn, seq, self.instant())
  transitions.py drain_dispatch:    now = store.instant()
                 resume_dispatch:   now = store.instant()

`instant()` REFUSES outside a write rather than falling back to `clock()`. A
caller with no open transaction is asking for wall time, not for this act's
instant; answering quietly with a fresh reading is exactly how the two drifted
apart in the first place. The refusal is witnessed by a regression.

Clearing in `finally` is not decoration. A leaked instant would be read by the
NEXT act as its own — the same defect with a longer window — and a write that
RAISED would leak it most easily. That is also witnessed.
