## [P2] One authority instant can still receive two transition timestamps

`drain_dispatch` samples `store.clock()` for `drain_requested` and the
singleton's draining transition. After the mutation returns,
`Authority._write` independently calls `self.clock()` again and passes that
new value into `_settle_dispatch` for `pause_reached` and the singleton's
paused transition. An empty drain deliberately records both control events at
one authority sequence because they are one indivisible committed instant,
but the timestamps can still disagree whenever the clock advances between
the two calls.

The correction's existing regression sets `BATON_WORK_NOW` to one constant,
so every sample is identical and it cannot distinguish “same clock source”
from “same act instant.” A ticking injected clock returning
`2099-01-02T03:04:05Z` and then `2099-01-02T03:04:06Z` produces the exact
split: the two events share one sequence but carry both timestamps, and the
singleton ends at the second.

Sample the authority instant once per write and reuse it for every control
transition that belongs to that act, or pass the mutation's already-sampled
instant through to settlement. Cover the immediate empty drain with a ticking
clock and retain the later-final-release case. The invariant is not merely
that both timestamps come from the same clock implementation; every event at
this indivisible authority instant must carry the same sampled instant.
