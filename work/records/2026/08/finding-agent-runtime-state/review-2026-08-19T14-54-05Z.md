## R14 — the stable start operation identity is paired with unstable operands

R12 is not yet satisfied. `RuntimePublisher` correctly reuses
`<incarnation>:start`, but `#reopen()` reconstructs the operation rather than
replaying the effective operands issued by `start()`:

- a caller-supplied `rationale` is replaced with the generic rationale; and
- a state-triggered reopen takes the state report's `session`, which may differ
  from the launch session.

The authority's effectively-once contract compares the effective operands as
well as `op-id`. In the ambiguous commit/result-loss case, both forms therefore
turn the intended replay into a mismatched-operation refusal and leave the
publisher believing that no lease exists even though the start may have
committed.

Fix the lease-opening operation as one immutable value at its first issue and
reuse that exact value for every explicit, scheduled, and state-triggered
retry. A later caller must not be able to alter any operand of that logical
operation.

Two additive regressions demonstrate both paths:

- `scheduled lease-start recovery preserves every effective operand`
- `state-triggered lease-start recovery preserves launch operands`

Focused result:

```text
npm test -- --test-name-pattern='lease-start|state-triggered'
8 passed, 2 failed
```

The failures show the exact `rationale` and `session` mismatches. R13's bounded
autonomous retry structure otherwise remains within the reviewed boundary.
