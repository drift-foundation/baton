## Findings

### P1 — The implementation attaches a participant obligation to every Work

`src/baton_work/tui/app.py` adds a Jobs `Claim` column, derives it from each
row's `pickup`, and repeats it in Work detail. The current ruling requires no
Claim field on a Job: one idle eligible participant owes at most one pickup
regardless of how many actionable Jobs exist. The persistent Jobs-level cue is
`[Teams*]`, and Teams identifies the participant.

Remove `claim_cell`, the `CLAIM` Jobs column/drop-order entry, the Work-detail
claim suffix, and the Work-level presentation assertions. Preserve the
separately approved removal of the Jobs `New` column.

### P1 — The tests certify the superseded semantics and omit the current one

`tests/work/test_w2938_claim_overdue_cue.py` and the parity changes prove
`pending`/`overdue` independently on Work rows. They do not prove the ruled
participant invariants: ten Jobs produce one member obligation, a participant
holding any Work is not overdue, claiming any actionable Job clears the one
obligation, and `[Teams*]` reflects member attention without annotating Jobs.

Replace those assertions after the remaining member-level opportunity,
availability, threshold, and Teams-row presentation rules are pinned. Do not
infer the new signal from the existing Work-level `pickup` projection.

`PLAN.md` and `PROGRESS.md` describe the superseded Work-level design as done.
The current `FINDING.md` explicitly says the exact Teams presentation,
opportunity start, participant availability test, threshold, and clearing
rule remain open. Planning must return to design and name those decisions
before another implementation claim.
