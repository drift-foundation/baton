=== [P1] THE RANGE PARSER ACCEPTED MORE THAN RFC 3339

  I delegated the grammar to datetime.fromisoformat and reasoned only
  about the one spelling I had in mind -- my own comment said 'Z is the
  spelling RFC 3339 blesses and fromisoformat accepts' and stopped
  there. 'Whatever this Python happens to parse' is not a contract a
  client can be held to or a future Python will keep.

  The grammar is now written out and checked BEFORE the parse:
    4094:_RFC3339 = re.compile(
    4095-	r"^\d{4}-\d{2}-\d{2}[Tt]\d{2}:\d{2}:\d{2}(\.\d+)?"
    4096-	r"([Zz]|[+-]\d{2}:\d{2})$")

  The parse still runs afterwards, because a string can match the shape
  and name no moment -- February 30th -- and a regex has no opinion
  about that. Both are covered, in both directions:
    test_time_bounds_accept_only_rfc3339_spelling   (reviewer's) refuses
    test_rfc3339_spellings_the_contract_does_accept accepts
    test_a_well_shaped_instant_that_names_no_moment_refuses

  AND THE FIX HAD ITS OWN DEFECT, which the accept-side case found.
  RFC 3339 §5.6 makes both T and Z case-insensitive, so
  2026-08-27t00:00:00z IS the contract -- and fromisoformat rejects the
  lower-case z. A grammar that admitted a spelling the parser then
  called 'not a moment' would have been worse than the original. The
  case is normalized before parsing, and the measurement showed only
  the z half is load-bearing on this CPython; the asymmetry is stated
  in the source rather than left to be rediscovered.
