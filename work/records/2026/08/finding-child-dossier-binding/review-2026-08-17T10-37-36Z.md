## Findings

### 1. Public `create` help advertises unbounded child depth

`src/baton_work/cli.py` describes the atomic creation binding as:

```text
work/records/YYYY/MM/<record>[/findings/<child>...]
```

The ellipsis says additional child levels are accepted, while the protocol
and validator allow at most two `/findings/<child>` segments. Use the same
exact bounded grammar already shown by `bind` so discovery does not direct an
operator into a guaranteed refusal.

### 2. Atomic creation and packaged CLI acceptance are not pinned

`tests/work/test_w309_child_bindings.py` exercises `bind_work()` directly
after Work creation. It does not exercise the observed locator through the
atomic `create binding=ROOT:path` path, and no packaged-archive test drives an
accepted child binding or a too-deep refusal. The dossier explicitly requires
packaged positive/negative evidence and exact JSON preservation.

Add focused atomic-creation coverage and a packaged CLI case that:

- creates Work with an accepted child or grandchild binding;
- reads it back byte-for-byte through packaged JSON;
- refuses a third child level without committing Work or binding state.

### 3. The new history assertion permits an impossible order

Binding history is canonically ordered by ascending revision. Replace the
`[GRANDCHILD, CHILD] or [CHILD, GRANDCHILD]` assertion with the one canonical
`[CHILD, GRANDCHILD]` order. Accepting either order weakens a deterministic
history contract and could hide a projection regression.
