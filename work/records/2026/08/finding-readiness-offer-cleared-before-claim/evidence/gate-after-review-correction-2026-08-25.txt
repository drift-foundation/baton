[P1] A FAILED CLAIMED-WORK RECOVERY WAKE IS NOT A CLAIMED OFFER

  `pending` was carrying two unrelated meanings:

    "an offer this adapter made that nobody has answered yet", and
    "a recovery wake for an already-claimed Work that nobody received".

  A claim answers the first. Nothing about it answers the second — and the
  `claimed:true` branch acknowledged both, so a participant whose one recovery
  wake failed sat on a live claim with no wake and no retry until a restart.

  The correction is a fourth status, `recovering`, created for a Work first
  seen ALREADY claimed and cleared only by its own successful delivery.
  `claimed:true` still acknowledges `pending` and `presented` — the
  acknowledgement this whole Work turns on is untouched.


[P1] ONE UNCLAIMED WORK BECOMES A TURN AT A TIME

  The review is exactly right about the asymmetry. In the ACP bridge
  `markPresented` runs AFTER `promptText` returns, so the head's claim-slot
  outcome is known and rotation is sound. In `codexBatonBridge` it runs when
  the socket accepts the event, before the dispatcher has started the turn.

  THE FIRST ATTEMPT WAS AT `enqueue`, AND IT WAS WRONG. Refusing a second
  unclaimed Work with `in-flight` while the target held one broke three
  existing retention cases:

    x  W99: Work B never starts on the context W A was interrupted in
           (completion-then-idle)  "the later Work was dropped rather than retained"
    x  W99: Work B never starts on the context W A was interrupted in
           (idle-then-completion)  "the later Work was dropped rather than retained"
    x  W99: an unrelated target keeps draining, for its own participant

  W99 requires the later Work to be QUEUED and held for a fresh context. The
  same review says not to weaken the quarantine retention paths, so refusing
  at the socket is the wrong boundary: it conflates "must not become a turn"
  with "must not be retained", and retention is what keeps the offer alive.

  SO THE GATE IS WHERE A DELIVERY BECOMES A TURN. The pre-turn revalidation
  each queued readiness event already performs answers a second question from
  the SAME canonical read:

    "over"      the episode is gone            -> dropped, as before
    "deferred"  the participant holds a claim  -> HELD at the queue head
    "live"      deliver it

  A deferred delivery is re-asked every `claimSlotRetryMs` (default 15s, a new
  config key). It is held and never dropped: a dropped offer would need a new
  episode to come back, which is this Work's own defect one layer down. A
  CLAIMED Work's own recovery delivery is never deferred, because it IS the
  claim.

  ASKED OF THE AUTHORITY RATHER THAN TRACKED LOCALLY, and that is the reason
  this is better than the socket refusal quite apart from W99: a claim taken by
  an interactive turn, by another adapter, or by an operator at a terminal is
  invisible to the dispatcher's own bookkeeping and exactly as occupying.

  ONE DIFFERENCE FROM THE REVIEW'S WORDING, STATED PLAINLY. It asks that B be
  "neither queued nor started". B may be queued here; it provably never starts.
  The harm the review names — "B can therefore spend a model turn against an
  occupied claim slot" — is closed, and the retention the same review protects
  is intact. If the reviewer wants socket-level refusal as well, that is a
  ruling about W99's retention contract and is theirs to make.
