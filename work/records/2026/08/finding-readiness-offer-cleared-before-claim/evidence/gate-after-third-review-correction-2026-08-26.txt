  `#revalidate`'s catch branch logged "the event is retained" and returned
  `live` — and `live` is the verdict that opens the model turn immediately and
  dequeues the event. The reply-with-no-actionable-set branch did the same.
  The sentence in the log had been true of the behaviour it described before
  the claim-slot correction, and stopped being true when that correction gave
  this read a second job.

  Because that is what changed. The immediate read now has to prove two
  things: that the exact episode still exists, and that an unclaimed Work is
  not waiting behind another claim. A READ THAT FAILED PROVES NEITHER.
  Delivering anyway spends a model turn against a slot this dispatcher never
  established was free — the exact thing the gate exists to stop — while ACP's
  equivalent failed read throws into its delivery boundary and retries without
  prompting.


THE CORRECTION: A FOURTH VERDICT, NOT A MAPPING ONTO AN EXISTING ONE

  `over` / `deferred` / `uncertain` / `live`. A failed invocation, unreadable
  JSON, or an envelope with no actionable set all answer `uncertain`, and the
  drain holds an uncertain event exactly as it holds a deferred one: the event
  stays at the queue head, its v11 in-flight identity is NOT released, no turn
  is spent, and it is re-asked on the same bounded cadence.

  ONE CADENCE FOR BOTH, and that is deliberate rather than lazy: "come back
  and ask again" is the same act whichever of the two questions went
  unanswered, and a second interval would be a second thing to tune for one
  behaviour.

  `actionDeferred` carries `reason: "unreadable"` for this branch, so an
  operator reading events can tell a slot that is occupied from an authority
  that could not be reached. They call for different actions.

  A deployment with no `roleInstructions` still answers `live` and is
  untouched: that is a deployment that never revalidates at all, not a read
  that failed, and the review scoped this to failed reads and malformed
  envelopes.
