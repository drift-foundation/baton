## Result

## Findings

### [P1] The Codex producer admits Work B while Work A's turn is still in flight

`ReadinessOffers.sync` in
`tools/codex-event-bridge/src/codex_baton_bridge.mjs` skips a presented Work
whose deadline has not arrived and continues scanning until it admits the next
pending Work (current lines 523–533). That is sound in the ACP bridge, where
`markPresented` runs only after the prompt returns and therefore the first
turn's no-claim outcome is known. It is not sound in `codexBatonBridge`, where
`markPresented` runs as soon as the socket accepts the event (current line
681), before the dispatcher starts or settles the managed turn.

The current state transition is directly reproducible without timing:

```text
snapshot [A unclaimed, B unclaimed] -> sync emits A
markPresented(A)                    -> A is backed off
same immediate snapshot            -> sync emits B
```

The exact reproduction against the current `ReadinessOffers` printed:

```json
{"first":["work:u-W1:1:g1"],"nextPoll":["work:u-W2:2:g1"]}
```

The producer loops immediately after an accepted emission because the
unchanged actionable set returns immediately and the zero-emission backoff
does not apply. The dispatcher retention in
`tools/codex-event-bridge/src/event_bridge.mjs` only refuses the **same event
id** (current lines 297 and 324), so B is accepted and queued behind A. If A
then claims, B remains in the participant projection beside claimed A and the
dispatcher's exact-key pre-turn revalidation still calls B live. After A's
turn completes, B can therefore spend a model turn against an occupied claim
slot.

This contradicts the confirmed “at most one unclaimed Work offer until its
claim-slot outcome is known” boundary and the updated README's “at most one
unclaimed Work is admitted at a time.” The producer test named `two unclaimed
Works yield only the canonical head until its outcome is known` currently
asserts A and then B on two immediate socket-level polls, so it protects this
gap rather than crossing the producer/dispatcher settlement boundary.

Required correction: keep later unclaimed Work out of the dispatcher while an
earlier unclaimed Work delivery is queued, starting, ambiguous, or active.
The regression should hold A's dispatcher turn before claim, poll the unchanged
`[A, B]` level repeatedly, and prove B is neither queued nor started. Then have
A claim and complete and prove B still cannot start until the canonical claim
slot actually frees. ACP's post-prompt rotation may remain as implemented.

### [P1] A failed claimed-Work recovery delivery is acknowledged without delivery

On the first snapshot of an already-claimed Work,
`ReadinessOffers.sync` creates a `pending` entry and emits the recovery wake
(current lines 493–501). If the ACP prompt or Codex socket delivery fails,
`markPresented` is deliberately not called. On the next unchanged snapshot,
however, the `claimed:true` branch changes either `pending` **or** `presented`
to `acknowledged` (current lines 502–509). The pending state here means the
recovery wake never reached the runner, not that an earlier unclaimed offer was
answered by a claim.

The exact reproduction against the current shared state, omitting
`markPresented` to model the failed delivery, printed:

```json
{"first":1,"second":0,"retained":false}
```

The corrected environment therefore receives no retry and the participant can
remain stranded on the live claim until another process restart—the same
restart-dependent failure mode this Work is meant to remove. Existing
forwarding/crash retry tests cover only `claimed:false`; the new claimed-Work
restart tests cover only successful delivery.

Required correction: distinguish an undelivered claimed-Work recovery wake
from a presented unclaimed offer. `claimed:true` may acknowledge the latter,
while the former must remain eligible until its own transport/prompt succeeds.
Add failed-then-repaired claimed-recovery regressions for both adapter paths
(or one direct shared-state regression plus an end-to-end assertion for each
delivery boundary).
