## Observed [P1] — lexical containment follows a symlink out of authority

`_roots` and `_mounts` use `os.path.normpath`, not filesystem-canonical
identity. A symlink created beneath the assignment workspace and pointing to a
directory outside every assignment root is accepted as a writable mount. The
closed argv hands the engine the symlink spelling, and the engine resolves it
to foreign host state. Positive root ownership must be proved after resolving
the actual host path (or by an equivalent no-follow identity mechanism), not
by lexical prefix.

Additive regression:
`AMountIsCanonicalAndNeverTheHosts.test_a_symlink_descendant_cannot_escape_an_assignment_root`.

## Observed — approved overlap refusals are absent

The ruling explicitly refuses ambiguous/overlapping roots and nested or
overlapping source/target spellings. The implementation currently:

- accepts `workspace` and `git` nested beneath `inputs`, leaving `_mounts` to
  classify by the first matching root;
- accepts one mount source nested beneath another source;
- accepts one container target nested beneath another target.

Those forms make writability authority ambiguous or let one mount hide/alias
another. Additive regressions:

- `test_assignment_roots_cannot_overlap`;
- `test_nested_mount_sources_and_targets_are_ambiguous` (source and target
  subcases).
