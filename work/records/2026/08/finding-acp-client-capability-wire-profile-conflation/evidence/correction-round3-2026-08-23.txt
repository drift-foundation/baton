5. THE REGRESSION I ADDED IS THE PROPERTY, NOT THE BRANCH

`records correction: acceptance MEANS the JSON document`. The finding was that
the rule has two directions, so asserting the new branch would be repeating
the mistake in test form -- I would be checking the direction I just noticed.
Instead it asserts the property against the GROUND TRUTH the proof stands in
for:

  recordFault(v, expected) === null  =>  the JSON document of v has EXACTLY
                                         those members

driven over five accepted spellings -- the empty document, the ACP document,
the same in the other insertion order, the same with no prototype, and a
document whose only member is `false` -- each checked by round-tripping
through `JSON.stringify` and comparing the actual wire keys.

THE CONVERSE IS DELIBERATELY NOT CLAIMED, and the case says so. A hidden EXTRA
member does not change the wire form either, and the proof refuses it anyway,
because a document carrying invisible state is not the document -- which is
what stops a non-enumerable `toJSON` from deciding the whole serialization.
The rule is stricter than the wire ON PURPOSE. Four refusal cases state that
edge explicitly: every expected member hidden, one of two hidden, a hidden
extra, and a hidden `toJSON`.

And it ends by showing the distinction itself: the hidden envelope's members
are READABLE and `JSON.stringify` of it is `{}`.

Both review regressions are retained unchanged.
