## [P0] `WorkspaceStorage` repeats the caller-held retarget boundary

`configured_workspace_storage` reads and cross-checks durable state, but then
copies the answer into `WorkspaceStorage.place`.  `attempt_custody_root` later
reads that caller-held slot.  The custom `__setattr__` is not a boundary:
`object.__setattr__(held, "place", unrelated)` replaces a slotted member
directly.  If the unrelated manager-owned directory contains
`attempt-1/workspace`, the mint accepts it and returns a `CustodyRoot` over that
unrelated tree.

This is the exact mechanism already ruled out in this dossier: its current
finding says that `object.__setattr__` reaches every slot, no representation
closes that, and the guarantee must come from not re-reading a caller-held
object.  Mirroring `WorkspaceGroup` therefore does not authenticate the root
at the later custody act; it copies durable authority back into the forbidden
process-state interval.

Required correction: the operation that selects the mount must re-open the
manager-owned durable allocation/configuration for the exact attempt, or
otherwise compose through an owner that does not accept/re-read a path-bearing
caller object.  Do not add another setter/private-slot/type-check defence.

## [P0] `CustodyRoot` can be retargeted after a valid mint

Even if the storage hop were fixed in isolation, `attempt_custody_root` copies
the resolved source into `CustodyRoot.place`, and `custody_vector` later reads
that slot without revalidation.  The same direct `object.__setattr__` changes a
genuine minted root to an unrelated host directory, after which the vector
places the replacement verbatim in `--mount source=...`.

Required correction: eliminate the second path-bearing handoff too.  The
durably authenticated lookup and final closed-vector composition need one
boundary, or the later operation must re-derive and re-authenticate the exact
attempt root instead of trusting the earlier object's path.
