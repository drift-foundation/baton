## [P0] A home alias redirects cleanup into a sibling attempt

`discard_execution_roots` starts with `os.path.isdir(home)`, which follows
links, reads mode with `lstat`, then calls `chmod(home)` and composes child
paths through that name. `_contained` canonicalizes each child and accepts it
when the resolved sibling remains inside the configured store. Containment
answers “inside this deployment”; it does not answer “owned by this attempt.”

The additive
`test_execution_cleanup_refuses_a_home_alias_to_a_sibling` replaces
assignment-1's removed home with a symlink to assignment-2's real home and
places a sentinel in assignment-2's workspace. Calling
`discard_execution_roots(..., "assignment-1")` deletes the sentinel. This is
the exact alias class `assignment_workspace` and custody derivation already
refuse, now reintroduced at the destructive boundary.

Prove the home and each surviving execution-root entry are real directories
at their exact canonical names, never links, before thaw or traversal. Keep
the proof and use on manager-owned identities without reopening an avoidable
alias interval. A broken link, replacement, wrong type or sibling target must
refuse without mutating either attempt; “inside the configured store” alone is
not sufficient authority to delete.
