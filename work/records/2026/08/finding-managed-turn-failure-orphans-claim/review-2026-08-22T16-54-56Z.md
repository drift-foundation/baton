## [P1] A late acknowledgement can suppress a successor orphan's incident

`EventBridge.#fileOrphanIncident` captures one `orphan` and publishes its
incident, but after the await it calls `#acknowledgeOrphanIncident(state)`.
That helper reads `state.orphan` again. The two references need not still name
the same fence.

This ordering is reachable without process corruption:

1. orphan A's incident publication remains in flight;
2. canonical reconciliation proves claim A released, clears its marker and
   drains retained readiness;
3. the successor managed turn fails holding claim B, creating orphan B and
   starting B's own publication;
4. A's publication finally succeeds, so its caller marks the current
   `state.orphan` — B — `incidentFiled=true` and saves B's marker;
5. B's publication returns false.

The resulting status and durable marker say B's incident was filed even
though it was refused. A restart trusts that false acknowledgement and does
not re-file, losing the actionable notice for the new orphaned claim.

The additive regression `a late acknowledgement cannot mark a successor
orphan filed` holds A and B's publications independently and reproduces the
ordering. It fails with B's `incidentFiled === true` instead of `false`.

Bind acknowledgement to the exact orphan object whose publication returned.
The old publication may acknowledge its captured object, but it must update
the live marker only when `state.orphan` is still that same object. Retain the
new regression alongside the concurrent join and false/throw retry cases.
