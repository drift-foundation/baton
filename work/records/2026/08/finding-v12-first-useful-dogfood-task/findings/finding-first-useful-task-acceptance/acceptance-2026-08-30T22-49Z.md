## Why the record cannot say WHY the worker was unable

It cannot, and that is a finding rather than an omission.

`result.json` -- 388 bytes of the agent's own account, the one artefact that
would say what the provider could not do -- was **discarded before any human
could read it**. `dogfood_operator._custody` hard-codes
`disposition="discard-after-intake"` (`tools/dogfood_operator.py:1227`); it is
not an operand and it does not read the operator's retention policy grant. The
manager supports three dispositions and two of them keep the material
(`intake.KEEPS_MATERIAL = ("retain", "quarantine")`); the operator never asks
for either.

The consequence is measurable in this record. The sealed result's own locator
names

    file:///.../custody/attempt-w39364-run2/proposal

and that directory does not exist. What survives custody is `sealed.json`
alone.

This makes two of W39364's own requirements unreachable through the documented
command as written:

- the acceptance criterion "A reviewer independently diffs the candidate
  against the measured input and runs `python3
  v12/spike/ping-pong/test_harness.py` outside the worker" -- there is no
  candidate left to diff or run;
- the assignment's own instruction to "retain correlated evidence and external
  candidate".

What this record retains instead is everything the manager's own frozen
documents carry: every file's size and content digest, the tree digest, the
completion-manifest digest, the freeze operation and its signature, the intake
receipt and the full journal. That is enough to prove the candidate was
unchanged -- which is what decides this REJECT -- and it is not enough to say
why the provider declined.

I did not re-run to recover the account. One supervised attempt was
authorized, one was taken, and a second would discard the account again for
exactly the same reason. The fix belongs before another attempt is worth
taking.
