# Design review — v12 isolated agent workers

Reviewer: `baton.claude` (implementer route, acting under the design review
checkpoint in `FINDING.md`). Work `W2` (`bcbb9dbf-W2`), claimed 2026-08-20.
Scope: critique only. No implementation, prototype, dependency, schema or
product-code change was made under this checkpoint.

Method: every decision in `FINDING.md` was re-read against the tree at
`bafc74f` plus this session's uncommitted W1568/W1578/W2571 changes —
`src/baton_work/transitions.py`, `projection.py`, `cli.py`, and
`tools/acp-baton-bridge/src/*.mjs`. Findings are labelled **Confirmed**
(measured in the tree), **Observed**, **Inferred**, or **Open**.

The design is coherent and unusually complete for a roadmap. The problems
below are gaps and contradictions, not objections to the direction. They are
ordered by how much rework they cause if found late.

---

## 1. The pinned "v11 cannot enforce claim-before-execution" text is now partly false

**Confirmed.** `FINDING.md`'s "Claim-gated execution — confirmed 2026-08-19"
says v11 "retains its documented claim-before-execution rule, but the shared
checkout cannot enforce it". As of 2026-08-20 that is true only of the
FILESYSTEM half. The protocol half closed today: `W2571`
(`finding-active-work-claim/findings/finding-pass-requires-current-claim`)
makes `pass` refuse unless the actor is the exact current claimant, so an agent
can no longer complete an assignment lifecycle without becoming Handler. The
W1568 incident this record's sibling cites — and the W93 slice-4 case cited
here — are no longer reachable through the public surface.

What remains unenforceable in v11 is narrower and worth stating precisely,
because it is the actual argument for v12: an agent can still READ, EDIT and
TEST without claiming. It simply cannot hand the result on or close it.

**Recommendation.** Append a dated clarification to this section rather than
rewriting it, narrowing the claim to filesystem writes and citing W2571. Left
as-is, the section overstates the v11 gap, and the first reader to check it
against the code will find the record wrong about its own motivating example.

## 2. "Assignment generation" has no v11 primitive, and the nearest one is wrong for the job

**Confirmed.** The design keys nearly everything on an "assignment
generation / claim epoch": capability minting, publication fencing,
stale-worker rejection, the claim-acceptance deadline, and the conformance
suite. v11 has three adjacent identifiers and none of them is it:

- `work.episode_seq` (`transitions._mint_episode`) is per-Work and
  authority-derived, and is exposed to readiness clients as
  `action_key = work:<id>:<episode_seq>`. But its docstring is explicit that
  claim deliberately does NOT mint: *"an episode is 'you have been handed
  this', not 'something about this changed'."* An episode therefore begins
  BEFORE the claim and does not change when the claim does.
- `runtime` leases carry an `incarnation`, but that is per-PARTICIPANT and
  operator-supplied, not per-assignment, and it survives across Work.
- the configuration `generation` is global and moves on `regen`.

So "the assignment generation that produced this candidate" is a NEW protocol
concept. That matters more than it looks: if a capability is keyed to
`episode_seq`, then a worker that inspects without claiming holds the same
epoch as the worker that later claims, and revoking one revokes the other.

**Recommendation.** PLAN item 1 should state explicitly that the assignment
generation is minted BY THE CLAIM and dies with it, that it is distinct from
`episode_seq`, `incarnation` and the config generation, and how it relates to
each. Getting this wrong is the single highest-rework item in the plan,
because items 2, 4, 6, 7 and 8 all carry it in their manifests.

## 3. "Read-only worker" and "the worker must claim" are in direct tension

**Confirmed contradiction.** The claim-acceptance section says a started worker
"receives only read-only inspection capability" and that the manager "denies
writable workspace and execution capabilities until that claim succeeds". But
`claim` is an authority MUTATION: it writes the `work` row and an event.

There are two different "write" boundaries here and the record uses one word
for both:

- authority writes (claim, say, poke-answer, release) — the worker must be
  able to make at least `claim` from its very first moment, or it can never
  satisfy the deadline;
- workspace and canonical-repository writes — genuinely deniable until the
  claim lands.

**Recommendation.** Split the vocabulary. State which authority verbs a
pre-claim worker may call (my reading of the intent: the read verbs plus
`claim`, and probably `say` and `poke-answer` so it can report an inability to
proceed), and say that everything else — including `pass`, `close`, `revise` —
requires the live capability. Without this the conformance invariant
"pre-claim execution is denied" is untestable, because "execution" is
undefined.

## 4. Nobody approves the proposal

**Confirmed gap.** The integration flow is specified end to end — worker
proposes, clean verifier certifies the exact candidate tree, integrator
compare-and-swaps the target — except for one step: *"It imports only an
approved proposal."* No section says who approves, on what authority, against
what, or where the approval is recorded. `baton.slaw` holds `approv` in this
deployment and `baton.codex` holds `rview`, so the roles exist; the design just
does not connect them.

This is not cosmetic. Verification proves the gates pass on the candidate
tree. It cannot evaluate whether the change is RIGHT, and it certainly cannot
evaluate the dossier prose in the same proposal (see §6). Something between
verification and integration has to be a judgement, and the record currently
leaves the integrator either rubber-stamping or silently acquiring a review
role it is explicitly told it does not have ("It does not resolve conflicts or
edit the proposal").

**Recommendation.** Add a named review/approval stage with its own authority,
its own disposition vocabulary, and its relationship to the existing `rview`
and `approv` roles — including how a reviewer READS an immutable proposal
(does the reviewer get a worker? a read-only clone with the proposal imported?
that is unspecified too).

## 5. `AGENTS.md` forbids exactly what the v12 worker must do

**Confirmed contradiction.** Repository policy is currently absolute: agents
never perform mutating Git operations, and the rule enumerates commit, merge,
rebase, cherry-pick, reset, checkout/switch, stash, branch/tag operations and
pushes.

The v12 worker commits, branches and rebases in its private clone by design.
The design's own justification is sound — those are proposal artifacts in a
disposable repository, not canonical history — but the policy text admits no
such distinction, and `AGENTS.md` names itself the one owner of that rule.

**Recommendation.** This must be an explicit dated supersession in `AGENTS.md`
scoping the prohibition to the CANONICAL checkout, landed as part of the v12
cutover rather than discovered by the first worker that tries to commit. Two
live contradictory rules is the failure mode `AGENTS.md` itself warns about.

## 6. The dossier layer is shared state that per-worker isolation does not isolate

**Observed, and I expect this to be the most frequent conflict class in
practice.** Workspace isolation solves concurrent edits to source. It does not
solve concurrent edits to `work/records/...`, which every assignment touches:

- `PROGRESS.md` has one writer per record, so parallel Work on the SAME record
  conflicts by construction;
- umbrella and parent `PLAN.md` files are indexed by child records, so two
  children progressing concurrently both edit the parent;
- `work/open/` is a directory of symlinks that every new record adds to.

None of these are code conflicts a rebase resolves cleanly, and all of them are
squarely in the "may the worker resolve this?" grey zone of §7. Two workers
adding different lines to the same parent plan is textually a conflict and
semantically not one.

**Recommendation.** Name the dossier as its own integration concern. Options
worth costing: keep records out of the proposal entirely and write them through
Baton rather than Git; make the integrator responsible for record merges; or
constrain record layout so concurrent writes are append-only per-file. This
does not need deciding now, but it should be an Open decision rather than an
unlisted one.

## 7. "Resolve the conflict only when clearly inside the Work contract" is the one unmechanical rule in the design

**Observed.** Every other invariant is machine-checkable, which is what makes
the conformance suite credible. This one delegates a judgement to a model, in
the exact place where a wrong call silently corrupts another agent's committed
work, and it is judged by the party with the least context about the other
change.

I am not arguing for removing worker rebases. I am arguing that "clearly
inside the contract" cannot be certified by the conformance suite, so it should
not sit beside invariants that can.

**Recommendation.** Either state a mechanical rule (for example: the worker may
rebase only when the rebase applies with zero conflicts, and reports blocked
otherwise), or state explicitly that this is a policy heuristic outside the
conformance boundary and name what catches a bad call — presumably review
(§4), which is another reason to specify it.

## 8. Two conformance invariants are stated more strongly than a suite can prove

**Observed.**

- *"credentials … cannot enter proposals, retained logs, or evidence."* A suite
  can prove credentials are absent from known locations and are not durable
  workspace content. It cannot prove absence from arbitrary worker output —
  that is secret scanning, which is heuristic.
- *"confirm quiescence before admitting a replacement attempt."* The record
  already handles the honest case ("If quiescence cannot be confirmed, the
  attempt is unhealthy/uncertain rather than silently replaced"), which is
  good — but the conformance bullet above it states quiescence as though it
  were always obtainable.

**Recommendation.** Weaken both to what is testable and say what carries the
residual risk. For credentials: non-persistence in the workspace, absence from
the proposal manifest, plus best-effort scanning of retained logs. For
quiescence: generation fencing is the invariant; confirmed quiescence is the
preferred path.

## 9. Runtime-profile probation has no v11 mechanism and interacts badly with routing

**Confirmed.** The design says deployment policy "may place that profile on
probation, remove it from automatic routing, or disable it until recertified".
In v11, routing is `kind -> route -> role + handlers` in `baton.json`, and
changing handlers means a config `regen` — a global generation bump that
affects every Work and every resolution snapshot. There is no notion of a
temporarily-disabled handler, and a profile (provider + model + adapter version
+ role instructions + policy) is not a routing concept at all: routes name
PARTICIPANTS.

**Recommendation.** State where profile state lives (deployment policy store,
not the routed configuration), and how a probated profile interacts with a
participant that is still a configured handler — is the Work simply not
offered, offered and unclaimable, or is the participant's runtime lease
refused? Each has different visible consequences in `teams`/`runtime`.

## 10. Time is load-bearing and unowned

**Open.** The claim-acceptance deadline, the runtime stop deadline, the grace
interval and the quiescence timeout are all wall-clock. The record never says
whose clock. In v11 the authority stamps its own times (`clock_ms_now`), the
runtime lease carries an `expires_at` supplied by the runner, and the worker
would be in a container with its own clock.

**Recommendation.** Name the manager's clock as authoritative for all worker
deadlines, and say what happens when a worker's own sense of time disagrees —
particularly that a worker must never conclude its own capability is still
live from a local timer.

## Positive revalidations

Worth recording, because they mean the design is buildable on what exists:

- **Confirmed.** The readiness envelope already carries `claimed` per
  actionable row (`projection.py`, the `wait`/participant projection), so a
  Worker Manager can observe claim state cheaply and authoritatively without a
  new read surface. The claim-acceptance deadline is implementable against
  today's projection.
- **Confirmed.** `action_key` is authority-derived, not a local poll counter,
  and `baton_readiness.mjs` already re-reads it immediately before a turn
  precisely because a queued prompt goes stale. The design's fencing instinct
  is the same one already implemented in the bridge.
- **Confirmed.** The ACP bridge already treats an unexpected permission request
  as a policy failure and cancels rather than auto-approving, and already
  refuses to assume session continuity the agent does not advertise. The
  "policy enforced outside the model" boundary has a working precedent.
- **Confirmed.** `release_claim` is already an exact compare-and-swap with a
  durable reason, and a gate arriving already releases a claimant atomically.
  The fenced-takeover sequence maps onto primitives that exist.

## Ordering risk

PLAN items 1-4 specify four interlocking artifacts (state machine, three
manifest schemas, ACP boundary, conformance suite) before anything runs. The
assignment-generation question in §2 and the capability-split question in §3
are both the kind that look settled on paper and change every schema once a
real worker claims, publishes, gets cancelled and comes back stale.

**Recommendation.** Insert a bounded end-to-end spike between items 1 and 2:
one container, one real assignment, claim → edit → propose → verify →
integrate, with cancellation and one deliberately stale worker. Throwaway code,
its only deliverable the corrections to items 1-4. The alternative is
discovering §2 and §3 in item 6.

Pre-existing, unrelated to this record, and not corrected under a
