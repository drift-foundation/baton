## The second review's seven findings are resolved

**A — participant capacity.** "Participant capacity" pins one live claim, one
assignment generation and one worker per participant, states that the Worker
Manager cannot widen it behind the authority, and makes "another certified
configured handler" the unit of route-level parallelism. `PLAN` 0m adds the
consequence I had not asked for and should have: competitive fan-out is a
coordinating parent plus one child Work per attempt, with selection naming the
exact winning child Work, generation and proposal. That closes the modelling
hole that one-slot capacity opens. Confirmed against the tree at
`src/baton_work/transitions.py:1420-1432`, and `W2938` is now closed
`satisfying` — the invariant the record leans on is accepted, not pending.

**B — two overlapping "nobody claimed" mechanisms.** Resolved better than I
proposed. Rather than ranking two clocks, the record removes one: the
manager-issued single-use claim token's expiry IS the claim-acceptance
deadline, and "there is no second manager deadline competing with it". The
authority's pickup obligation is named the broader operator-facing signal, a
token expiry is journaled as one handoff's `offer-expired` evidence, and it
neither creates a second overdue state nor clears or restarts pickup age.

I checked each of those claims against the W2938 implementation rather than
accepting them:

- "A participant already using its one live claim slot does not incur another
  pickup obligation" — `_sweep_pickup` clears the interval for a busy member
  (`transitions.py:601-638`), and `member_pickup` returns `state: None` for
  busy, empty-pool or ineligible members (`projection.py:2768-2792`).
- "one obligation however deep the queue" — matches the docstring and the
  one-row-per-participant table.
- "a token expiry neither clears nor restarts pickup age" — an expiry changes
  neither claim state nor pool membership, and the sweep leaves an existing
  row alone while the pool stays continuously non-empty, so the age keeps
  running. Re-offering the same unclaimed Work to a different eligible
  participant does not disturb the first participant's interval either.
- "only a successful claim, or a routing/lifecycle change that removes the
  obligation, satisfies it" — the sweep's three clearing conditions are
  exactly busy, empty pool, and no longer eligible.

The design and the shipped authority agree.

**C — `plan-rejected` could loop.** Resolved: rejection now atomically places
the Work behind a typed `plan-revision` scheduler gate, the plan owner
satisfies the gate only after publishing an accepted immutable revision, and
any retry takes a new claim generation. The loop I described is closed at its
cause — schedulability — rather than by a retry counter.

**D — plan status ownership.** Resolved for v12: `PLAN.md` never carries
mutable execution status and `PROGRESS.md` is the separate generation-attributed
journal. See item 4 below for the transition clause this ruling still needs.

**E — an isolated worker could not file a discovery.** Resolved: an idempotent
manager-mediated `file-discovery` intent, canonical child Work returned by the
manager, a typed `record-materialization` gate so a schedulable Work can never
exist without its record, no claim authority granted by filing, and the current
assignment continuing unless the worker explicitly reports a blocker. This
preserves `AGENTS.md`'s recursive role-neutral discovery inside the sandbox.

**F — compact identity forms.** Resolved and generalized beyond what I raised:
compact Work, message, topic and assignment labels are presentation-only, never
persisted as identity, never authoritative manifest fields, and never resolved
implicitly by a mutating operation.

**G — spike coverage.** Resolved. The spike now names expired-token failure and
recovery, `plan-rejected` gating, a real replay conflict that stops, four
distinct verification/review/approval/integration actors, competitive fan-out,
stale-generation fencing, and an explicit list of what it does NOT settle.

## Remote-adapter requirement — pinned consistently

The new "Remote worker adapters" section, the expanded spike, and `PLAN` 0v/6
agree: the same worker-control contract, adapter mechanics excluded from
protocol vocabulary, manager-held authority, token bound to the exact remote
runtime attempt, digest-bound inputs and proposals, transport loss treated as
uncertain quiescence with fencing first, reattachment only on positive proof of
the same runtime and generation, and — the operative clause — the non-local leg
runs inside the bounded spike after the local OCI skeleton and **before**
worker-control and manifest schemas freeze, with a loopback simulation
explicitly not sufficient. I found no surviving text anywhere in the record
that still defers remote execution to later production conformance; the earlier
deferral is marked superseded in place.

## Residual items

### 1. The conformance suite does not require the remote-adapter properties

**Decision-ready.** The record defines compliance narrowly: a container, VM or
remote worker "is compliant only when the same conformance suite proves all of
these invariants", followed by a fixed list. That list — and `PLAN` item 4 —
covers isolation, generation-gated publication, cancellation and quiescence,
pre-claim denial, credential non-retention, policy, untrusted output and
lifecycle normalization. It does not cover the properties the remote section
itself declares mandatory: transport loss is not proof of a stopped process,
fencing precedes uncertain-quiescence recording, and reattachment requires
positive proof of the same runtime and generation.

So an SSH worker could pass the suite as written while never being tested on
the failure mode that distinguishes it from a local one. The spike exercises it
once; the suite is what certifies every later runtime.

**Recommendation.** Add transport-partition fencing and proof-bound
reattachment to the conformance invariant list and to `PLAN` item 4. This is
the one item I would resolve before the spike, because the spike's traces are
supposed to seed the suite and they will only seed what the suite claims to
require.

### 2. No post-claim deadline scenario exercises the policy digest

**Decision-ready.** The deadline ruling gives post-claim expiry real machinery:
expiry is evidence by default, only the route policy pinned into the
assignment's policy digest may authorize an automatic action, and that action
must name its deadline and policy clause, compare-and-swap the current
assignment generation, journal the result, and preserve or quarantine
recoverable work.

The spike exercises the pre-claim token deadline (which is self-enforcing, so
no policy clause is involved) and an explicitly requested cancellation. It
never exercises a deadline-driven policy action. That leaves the pinned policy
digest, the clause-naming requirement and the generation compare-and-swap
unexercised — and the policy digest is a manifest field that is about to
freeze.

**Recommendation.** Add one post-claim scenario to the spike, most cheaply a
cancellation-grace force-stop, since cancellation is already staged: it is the
one case the ruling says may force-stop, and it puts a policy clause, the
digest and the CAS on a live timer before the manifests become compatibility
promises.

### 3. One surviving unqualified sentence about parallel child Work

**Decision-ready, one clause.** `FINDING.md:314` still reads "Parallel child
Work remains safe because each child owns a distinct claim, proposal, and
dossier", while "Participant capacity" now says it is "safe only across those
distinct participants". Both are live and the second is the true one.

`AGENTS.md` is explicit that this is the failure mode to avoid: "Two live rules
that contradict each other are worse than either alone, because both look
authoritative."

**Recommendation.** Qualify the older sentence in place — distinct
participants, each with its own claim — or mark it clarified by the capacity
ruling. No new reasoning required.

### 4. Plan immutability needs the transition clause the Git ruling got

**Decision-ready.** `AGENTS.md:47` currently REQUIRES the opposite of the v12
rule: a confirmed decision must have "its queued/in-progress/done state" in the
applicable `PLAN.md`. Every record in the tree follows it, including this
dossier's own `PLAN.md`, whose items the approver marked `[done]` while pinning
"`PLAN.md` never carries mutable execution status".

That is not a defect in the ruling — it is the same shape as the Git-mutation
boundary, which the record handled explicitly: the blanket rule stays in force
until enforcement exists, and "the repository policy and enforced worker cutover
must change together so the future exception cannot be misread as permission in
today's shared checkout". The plan-status ruling has no such clause, so today a
reader can find one live rule requiring plan status marks and another
forbidding them, with no stated cutover between them.

**Recommendation.** Add the matching sentence: the v11 `AGENTS.md` convention
stands until the v12 cutover, at which point `AGENTS.md` and the
`PLAN`/`PROGRESS` split change together.

### 5. `file-discovery` only produces child Work

**Minor, decision-ready.** The ruling has the manager commit "canonical child
Work". `AGENTS.md` gives the filer a choice: a causally tied child record, or
"a top-level record instead when it is independent, separately scheduled, or
may outlive the parent". Both discoveries filed from inside other Work this
week (`W2645`, `W2693`) were independent, not children.

Either answer works and the cost is one sentence. Say that a worker's filing
always starts as a child and promotion to top level is a trusted-tooling
decision (`AGENTS.md` already defines promotion as a new top-level record with
a forwarding note), or let the intent carry the child/independent
classification as a proposal the manager may honour.

### 6. "the exact current attempt" is ambiguous under the token model

**Minor, precision only.** `FINDING.md:623-627` says the suite proves the
claim-acceptance deadline, ending with "an explicit pinned policy can cancel
and fence the exact current attempt". Under the token model a pre-claim expiry
has no assignment generation, Handler or writable workspace to fence — only a
runtime attempt to clean up — while policy-authorized cancellation belongs to
post-claim deadlines. The sentence reads as though one mechanism covered both.

**Recommendation.** Split it: pre-claim expiry is self-enforcing and its
consequence is runtime cleanup and re-offer policy; post-claim expiry is where
a pinned policy clause may cancel and fence the current assignment generation.

## Disposition

No blocking objection, and no contradiction between the newly pinned rulings
themselves. Items 3, 4 and 6 are text corrections that make the record say what
it already means; item 5 is a one-sentence choice; items 1 and 2 are scope
decisions about the conformance suite and the spike, and item 1 is the one I
would settle first because the suite outlives the spike.

none was performed.
