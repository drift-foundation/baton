1. WHY THE LOOP HAPPENED, IN ONE SENTENCE

My inventory came from recall and my probes proved that a refusal happened
rather than proving WHICH boundary refused -- so each round I fixed the sites a
review named, claimed a sweep, and the same class survived where I had not
thought to look.

Both halves are now mechanical.


2. THE LAYER: `boundaries.py`, FIVE KINDS

  text        exact, non-empty, encodable. Keeps a value out of SQLite's way.
  identity    the same rule, for a value a durable ROW IS NAMED BY. Named apart
              because a lookup for an identity that cannot exist is a different
              mistake from storing text that cannot be stored -- and because the
              inventory needs a vocabulary to be legible in.
  instant     text, the frozen grammar, AND A REAL CALENDAR MOMENT.
  deadline    an instant plus a duration, with the duration and the SUM inside
              the domain.
  injected    the answer of a capability trusted deployment supplied.

EVERY REFUSAL NAMES ITS BOUNDARY. `what` is not decoration: it is how a probe
proves it arrived, and it is the mechanism that makes vacuity detectable.

THREE PROPERTIES, NOT ONE, FOR AN INSTANT. Fixed-width digits do not establish a
calendar -- `2026-99-99T99:99:99.999Z` had the shape, escaped `strptime` as a raw
`ValueError` on the arithmetic path, and on the comparison path was never parsed
at all: it simply sorted after every real deadline and silently expired a live
offer. AND THE CALENDAR DOES NOT ESTABLISH THE GRAMMAR: `2026-8-24T0:0:0.1Z` is
a real moment, parses cleanly, and SORTS WRONG -- "2026-8-24" orders after
"2026-12-01". Neither implies the other, so the owner establishes both, and a
case reads all three refusals to check they say which property failed.


3. THE INVENTORY IS DERIVED, NOT RECALLED

`test_boundary_inventory.py` walks the AST of the manager packages, collects
every `boundaries.<kind>(..., "<label>")` call, and attributes each to every
exported operation that can REACH it -- transitively, through the package's own
helpers. Ten operations, twenty-eight distinct boundaries, none of them listed
by hand.

  a boundary added anywhere              appears in the derived set and fails
                                         until it is probed
  a helper that newly reaches one        changes the attribution, because the
                                         walk is transitive
  a boundary call with no literal label  RAISES rather than being skipped, so
                                         the inventory cannot lose one quietly

THE DERIVATION FOUND TWO THINGS ON ITS FIRST RUN, and both are exactly what it
is for:

  A HAND-WRITTEN RULE BESIDE THE LAYER. `_agreeing` had its own copy of the text
  rule whose message happened to say "an operation signature" -- so a probe aimed
  at that label was refused by a rule the inventory could not see. Through the
  layer now.

  A LOOP THAT HID ITS LABELS. `issue_offer` proved seven operands with one call
  inside a `for` over a table of (value, label) pairs. It reads well and it is
  invisible to a walk. UNROLLED: a rule that is applied and cannot be SEEN to be
  applied is a rule the next reviewer has to take on trust.


4. EVERY PROBE PROVES IT ARRIVED

Each probe establishes real preconditions -- issuing an offer, accepting one --
and then asserts the EXACT LABEL of the boundary it targets. A probe refused
earlier by a precondition sees a different label and fails.

`test_a_probe_that_is_refused_earlier_fails` proves that guard rather than
promising it: it drives the review's own vacuous shape (a spoiled clock on an
offer that does not exist) and requires the harness to reject it.

AND WHAT CANNOT BE PROBED IS DECLARED WITH ITS REASON. Five derived boundaries
have no caller operand that can reach them -- the from-instants inside `deadline`,
whose callers prove the instant earlier, and `deadline`'s own answer. Each is
listed with why, and `test_every_declared_unreachable_is_really_unreachable`
CHECKS the claim by spoiling the operand that would reach it and requiring the
earlier boundary to refuse first. An unreachable entry is a claim a reviewer can
check and a later cut can invalidate; it is not an opt-out.
