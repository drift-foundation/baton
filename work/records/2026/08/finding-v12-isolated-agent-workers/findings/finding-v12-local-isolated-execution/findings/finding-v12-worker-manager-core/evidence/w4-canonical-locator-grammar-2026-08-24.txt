  THE GRAMMAR, enforced over the ORIGINAL TEXT with no parse and no
  reconstruction anywhere in it:

    scheme      lower-case ASCII `[a-z][a-z0-9+.-]*`
    shape       `scheme://authority` then nothing or an absolute path;
                `file:///absolute-path` with no remote authority
    authority   no userinfo; one non-empty lower-case DNS/IPv4 host or one
                bracketed IPv6 literal; an optional port 1 to 65535
    everywhere  no query, no fragment, no backslash, no control or space
    path        percent escapes are `%` and two UPPER-CASE hexadecimal digits

  The IPv6 literal is the one place a library decides: `ipaddress` in Python and
  the constructor's own hostname round-trip in Node. Hand-rolling an address
  parser is what the ruling steers away from, and BOTH sides additionally
  require the literal to be lower case and to come back as the SAME TEXT -- an
  address that only becomes valid after normalization is not canonical.

  `urllib` LEAVES the Python allowlist and `ipaddress` joins it. The grammar
  needs no URI parser at all now, which is a smaller surface than the widening
  I asked for two rounds ago.
