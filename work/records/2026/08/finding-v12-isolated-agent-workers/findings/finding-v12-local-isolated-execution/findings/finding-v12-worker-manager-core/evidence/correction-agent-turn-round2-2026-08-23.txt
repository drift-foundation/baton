2. A SCAN OVER A PROJECTION IS A SCAN OVER THE PROJECTION

[P1] THE TURN BODY COULD DURABLY RETAIN A LIVE BEARER. The operation journal
scans the RESULT it commits — and that summary omits `evidence` and
`adapter_diagnostics`, which is exactly where a bearer would sit. So a live
bearer under an innocently named diagnostic landed in `turns.body` while the
journal committed a clean summary and reported no leak.

I had reasoned that the journal's durable-secret boundary covered this. It
covers what it is GIVEN. `assertNoDurableSecret` now runs over the exact owned
document before anything can be inserted or journalled, and a refusal leaves
both tables untouched.


3. A SEAL THE CONSUMER DOES NOT CONSULT PROTECTS NOBODY

[P1] THE DISPOSITION GATE TRUSTED AN UNSEALED SUMMARY COLUMN. I added a sealed
canonical record and then left the one safety decision reading
`turns.permitted` directly — so editing that single column made the manager
accept `completed` from a refused turn while the retained record still forbade
it.

The gate consumes the sealed record now. And the summary must AGREE with it:
a drifted query column is an integrity failure wherever it is found rather
than something to quietly prefer the sealed side of, because the next reader
may be one that only has the column.

`turnRecordOf` also binds a FOURTH witness — the identity the caller asked
for. The declared digest, the recomputed digest and the stored digest can all
agree while the record answers to somebody else's turn id, which is what
copying one row's body and seal onto another produces.
