3. THE ONE GAP I FOUND BY PROBING MY OWN CUT

A `receipt_id` REUSED -- across kinds on one proposal, or across two proposals
-- hit the table's uniqueness and left as `IntegrityError`. That is a FAULT: it
takes the whole transaction down and journals nothing, so the caller got an
unexplained crash instead of "that identity is taken".

`publish` already had exactly this rule for proposal identities. The receipts
did not. Same omission, neighbouring transition -- which is the shape I have now
been shown four times, so I looked for it deliberately and it was there.

Receipt identities are claimed once now, and the refusal names the holder.

THREE ZEROS, AND EACH GOT A DIFFERENT ANSWER -- which is the useful part.

  R15 and R18 were MISSING COVERAGE, and they were missing for the same reason:
  the approval check is SHADOWED by the review check that fires before it, so
  three cases mentioned "integration requires explicit approval" and none of
  them ever reached that line.  One case -- verification passed, review
  accepted, and nothing else -- witnesses both, and it also pins that this
  refusal is ORDINARY (nothing journalled, identity still usable) and that a
  DENIED approval is not an approval.

  R5 was a GENUINE REDUNDANCY, and I removed it rather than reporting it.  A
  separate "is it missing" check in front of `check_text` changed neither the
  verdict nor the message -- `check_text` already names the member -- so keeping
  it would have been a guard nothing can observe.  Collapsed to one mechanism;
  mutating THAT fails sixteen cases.

  The lesson I am taking is that "no witness" has three distinct answers --
  missing case, redundant code, real equivalence -- and the last two rounds I
  reached for the third one first.
