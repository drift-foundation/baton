1. THE OPERAND CUT 2 PROMISED, WITH THE MECHANISM THAT GIVES IT MEANING

Cut 2 deliberately did not take an `operation_id`, on the grounds that an
operand accepted with no mechanism behind it is a claim rather than a property.
Cut 3 adds both together: every mutating transition now REQUIRES one and runs
through `Store.replay`, so an exact repeat replays the first outcome and a
reused id with different operands collides.

Seven transitions were re-signatured, as cut 2's hand-back said they would be.


2. WHAT THE JOURNAL GUARANTEES, AND WHERE EACH RULE LIVES

  four states          no row is UNSUBMITTED; the others are COMMITTED,
                       REFUSED and RETIRED, and one identity is durably in
                       exactly one of them
  the savepoint        an ordinary refusal rolls back to it and writes nothing;
                       a DURABLE refusal releases it, keeps what the action
                       wrote, and records the refusal so the retry REPLAYS it;
                       a fault that is not a Refusal takes the whole
                       transaction down, because an operation whose failure we
                       cannot describe is not one we may record an outcome for
  the order            RETIREMENT is answered BEFORE the signature, because
                       retirement is a property of the operation IDENTITY.  A
                       stale submitter must learn the identity is dead, not
                       that its operands disagree -- those are different facts
                       and only one of them is true
  the signature        the FULL effective operands including the prose, so
                       reusing one id with different durable text is a refusal
                       rather than a silent replay of somebody else's result
  the claim signature  names the WORK as well as the participant: this
                       authority holds many Works, and an id meaning "claim by
                       this participant" would collide across them
  settlement           NOT lookup-then-write.  One transaction that either
                       finds the committed result or retires the identity so
                       nothing can ever commit under it again
  may_retire           defaults to FALSE.  The frozen host defaulted it true,
                       so omitting the operand retired an unsubmitted claim on
                       the spot; settlement authority is asserted, never
                       inherited by saying nothing
  the bound disposition  stops the next caller, on whatever entry path, from
                       relabelling a settlement timeout as a refused claim
  the lookup           RAISES when the authority cannot answer, because "I
                       could not ask" must never be read as "it did not
                       commit".  An unanswerable lookup settles nothing


3. THE ONE FAULT-INJECTION SEAM, AND WHY IT EXISTS

`set_lookup_available` is the only seam in the module, and it is there because
§8 turns on the difference between "it did not commit" and "I could not ask".
A store or transport fault has to be reachable in a test or the rule that an
unanswerable lookup settles NOTHING is unprovable. It affects only
`operation_result`. It lives on the private `Core`, not on a public face.


4. THE DURABLE REFUSAL IS PROVED WHERE IT LIVES

No cut-3 TRANSITION raises a durable refusal -- the stale-target integration
that does is cut 4. So the savepoint mechanism is exercised directly through
`Store.replay` with an action that writes and then refuses durably: its writes
survive, the refusal is bound to the identity, and the retry REPLAYS the
refusal rather than appending a second attempt. The ordinary-refusal and
non-Refusal-fault paths are exercised the same way.

Proving the mechanism where it lives, rather than waiting for cut 4, is the
point: otherwise cut 4 arrives on an untested savepoint.


5. THE RACES ARE REAL PROCESSES, AND THE BARRIER IS REAL

Three children, spawned, against one store file, because a competing claim has
to lose by being REFUSED inside a transaction -- which is a decision -- rather
than by failing to get a transaction at all, which is an error. Two threads in
one interpreter would share a connection and prove neither.

MY FIRST BARRIER WAS A HEAD START, NOT A BARRIER. The parent wrote a start file
after spawning, so children merely began at different times and the "race" was
a sequence with extra steps. Each child now announces that it is loaded and
connected, and the parent releases them only once it has seen EVERY one.

  competing claims        one winner, two reasoned losers.  Every loser's
                          refusal names a claim reason and none of them says
                          "locked" -- the assertion is on WHY they lost, not
                          just that they did.  The child reports are printed
                          into the failure message, so a faulted child says so
                          rather than hiding inside a count.
  one fixed operation id  ALL THREE SUCCEED, and that is the point: same
                          operands, same id, so the later arrivals REPLAY the
                          first outcome rather than being refused for a race
                          they did not lose.  Exactly one claim event exists
                          afterwards and the counter is 1.
  a claim against a
  settlement              BOTH ORDERS ARE CORRECT and the case asserts each on
                          its own terms: either the claim committed and the
                          settlement observed it, or the settlement retired the
                          identity first and NOTHING committed under it.  What
                          must never happen is a third story.  Run repeatedly,
                          both orders occur -- observed 4 committed and 2
                          retired over six runs -- so the case is exercising a
                          real race rather than a fixed outcome.


6. TWO GAPS I FOUND BY PROBING MY OWN CUT

  a) A 1 MB OPERATION ID BECAME A DURABLE PRIMARY KEY. `check_text` bounds
     nothing. An operation id IS an opaque identity and the frozen contract
     already has a grammar for those -- `opaqueId`, 160 characters and a fixed
     grammar -- so this REUSES it rather than inventing a limit. Every id this
     authority's callers use, and every UUID, satisfies it. Stated in one place
     (`opaque_id_fault`) so a second boundary cannot reach a different
     conclusion about the same string.

  b) A LYING CLOCK NOW STOPS A TRANSITION BEFORE THE JOURNAL IS TOUCHED. The
     instant is taken before `replay`, so a clock answering the wrong shape
     refuses without leaving an operation row stamped with nonsense -- and the
     id stays usable, because an ordinary refusal writes nothing.

AND ONE MEASURED, NOT FIXED, AND RAISED HERE: the settlement `signature` is
caller-supplied durable text with no bound. A 1 MB signature is accepted and
written on retirement (measured: `durable signature length: 1000000`). I did not
invent a bound, because the authority PRODUCES signatures whose length follows
the operands, and a limit shorter than one the authority can produce would make
a legitimate settlement impossible. The frozen contract states no bound for it.
Raised for a ruling; a natural answer is to bound it once cut 5 exposes
settlement to a participant, since cut 3's only callers are trusted.
