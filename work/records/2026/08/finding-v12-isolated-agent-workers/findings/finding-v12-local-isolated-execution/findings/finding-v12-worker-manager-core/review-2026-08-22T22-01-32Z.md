## Findings

### P1 — concurrent exact issue still returns a mismatched secret

`issueOffer` checks replay before minting (`offers.mjs:187`), but the deciding
replay remains inside `ControlStore.transact`, after minting
(`offers.mjs:194-196`). Two exact issuers can both observe the optimistic miss.
The winner commits its verifier; the loser then receives the winner's replayed
record from `transact` and returns it beside the loser's newly minted bearer.
This is the original unusable bearer/verifier pair under the concurrency that
the operation journal exists to settle. The loser must refuse after the
transaction decides replay, without emitting its bearer.

Additive regression: `W2929 re-review: concurrent exact issue never pairs the
loser's bearer`.

### P1 — TTL does not release an offer whose worker never answers

The only call to `expireOffer` is the late-decision branch of `acceptOffer`
(`offers.mjs:247-257`, `343-353`). If no decision arrives, an elapsed row stays
`issued`, its verifier remains unspent, and the partial unique index
(`schema.mjs:94-96`) refuses a replacement forever. Expiry must be reachable
from manager-owned time/reissue/recovery processing, not depend on the holder
of the expired authorization sending one more message.

Additive regression: `W2929 re-review: TTL expiry releases the Work without a
late decision`.

### P1 — the issue signature omits its durable authority binding

The offer journal signature (`offers.mjs:175-178`) includes the local Work id
but not the persisted `authority_uuid` (`offers.mjs:199-207`). Reusing an issue
identity against another authority therefore raises the sequential exact-
reissue refusal instead of `refused.operation-collision`. Include the full
authority-scoped Work binding in the effective signature; a changed durable
operand is not an exact replay.

Additive regression: `W2929 re-review: the authority binding rides the issue
signature`.
