1. A FOUR-PART IDENTITY IS NOT OWNED IF ONE RELATIONSHIP IS ONLY SHAPED

  Last round I made `_assignment` own each part of an assignment identity and
  compare the two I thought were relationships -- the Work and the participant.
  `authority_uuid` I typed as durable text and left unrelated. So a perfectly
  well-formed assignment from authority ffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffff was
  accepted for an offer issued from 0000000000000000000000000000000a, advanced
  the offer to `claimed`, and recorded the foreign generation.

  THREE of the four parts are relationships, not two. The authority is now
  supplied from the offer's own adopted row and compared on both paths -- the
  direct claim and the late commit -- because they are the same document
  arriving twice and correcting one of them is this campaign's recurring defect.

  The port's `claim` and `settle_operation` take the authority beside the Work,
  and the inventory records both as stated relationship operands with a witness.

2. A MEMBER SET IS NOT A FIELD CONTRACT -- THE OTHER TWO PLACES

  The same sentence from the last review, applied where I had not applied it.

  THE RETIREMENT'S BOUND RECORD. `reason` and `disposition` were checked for
  PRESENCE. The manager records the one and BRANCHES on the other -- one reaches
  a TEXT column, the other decides which control state is written -- so an
  integer reason was adopted as the terminal decision and the offer settled.
  Both are owned as text now.

  THE SEALED REFUSAL. `Column.members` was a tuple of NAMES, so a persisted
  refusal whose category was the integer 7 passed the row contract; replay then
  handed it to `ContractRefusal`, whose closed-pairing check is an ASSERTION
  about this build's own raising sites, and a caller replaying its first answer
  got an AssertionError.

  A new `refusal` column kind owns the whole seal at its one adopted crossing:
  the four members, the §9 CLOSED PAIRING between category and code, the text
  message and the durable marker. What makes a seal a seal is the pairing --
  `refused.precondition` and `policy.retention` carry different portable
  meanings and different retry policies, so a pair this build cannot place is
  not one of its refusals however well-formed its parts.

3. THE UNIVERSE NOW FOLLOWS A CROSSING WHEREVER IT IS SPELLED

  The reviewer's second P1 was that the model treated semantic fields as
  container presence, and the gap was in the ORIGIN TRACKING rather than in any
  one owner: it stopped at `bound = answer.get("record") or {}`, at a private
  helper's RETURN, and at a list of rows.

  There were two tracking functions and they had drifted -- the one that bound
  local names followed some shapes and the one that resolved a call's argument
  followed others -- so a crossing handed straight INTO a helper was tracked and
  the same crossing handed back OUT of one was not. They are one function now,
  `_source`, which follows: reads, capability calls and values, the port's
  answers, the layer's own returns, member reads, subscripts, boolean and
  conditional defaults, comprehensions of rows, and private helpers' returns to
  a fixpoint.

  ADOPTED COLUMNS ARE ENTRIES. An adopted origin now names the READ as well as
  the table, because `meta` is read at two places and a crossing belongs where
  it happened. A column that is read is its own entry, owned by the row contract
  that covers it, with its own probe -- 29 adopted entries where there were 5.

  AND THE PROBES FOR THEM ARE DERIVED FROM THE TABLE CONTRACTS rather than
  listed, because a column added to a contract and never spoiled is the gap this
  file keeps being corrected for. A probe writes what SQLite itself would
  refuse, with `PRAGMA ignore_check_constraints` on the side connection: a CHECK
  binds THIS build's writers, and adopted data is by definition data this
  process did not write.

  THE ANTI-CIRCULARITY CHECK THIS CREATED. Generating the column probes from the
  universe means both shrink together if the tracking stops working -- exactly
  the shape of every finding in this campaign, arriving in my own fix. So there
  is a SECOND mechanism: a flat scan for `x["<name>"]` where the name is a
  column of a table this build owns, using none of the tracking, compared
  against what the tracking found. Three mutations that disable tracking paths
  are caught by it and by nothing else.
