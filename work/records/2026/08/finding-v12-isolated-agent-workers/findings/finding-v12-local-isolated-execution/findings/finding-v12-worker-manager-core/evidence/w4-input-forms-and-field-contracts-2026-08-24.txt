THE SHAPE OF THIS ROUND'S FINDING

  Last round I made the universe structural for the domain a review had
  demonstrated, and this round says the structure I chose was still a SUBSET of
  the language. `receiving_entries()` enumerated ordinary positional and
  keyword-only parameters, capability calls and SELECT statements -- three of
  the forms a value can arrive in, presented as all of them.

  What fell through the gaps was not hypothetical:

    - an unencodable bound participant constructed happily, because the
      inventory saw the session's CALLS and never `session.participant`;
    - a non-callable bearer mint performed a Work projection, a certification
      check, expiry processing and a capacity read before escaping as a raw
      TypeError -- and it was excluded from the universe BY NAME, on the
      grounds that capabilities are owned at their constructors, while no
      constructor owned it;
    - nine public constructors take `**members` and were absent from an
      inventory that claimed to hold every public parameter.

  An exclusion nobody checks is a hole with a comment over it. That is the
  sentence I want to keep from this round.

1. EVERY SIGNATURE FORM, EVERY CAPABILITY VALUE, EVERY CAPABILITY OPERAND

  `_parameters()` reads all four forms, so the nine `**members` constructors are
  entries. `NOT_INPUTS` is now {self, cls}: a capability OPERAND is an entry like
  any other, and what differs is who owns it.

    typed at their own site   mint_bearer (issue_offer), action (transact),
                              clock (open), claim_signature (AuthorityPort)
    typed at construction     session -- each of the four operations it must
                              carry, before an offer can spend anything
    named to a constructor    store, port -- and CONSTRUCTED_BY has to name a
                              site that EXISTS and owns something, which is
                              checked rather than assumed

  A capability's bound VALUE is its own entry: `session.participant` is owned as
  durable text at construction. The label carries the reason ("the identity this
  session binds an authorization to"), because a refusal here should say what is
  actually lost rather than restate the rule.

  `__init__` is treated as public whatever its leading underscore. A constructor
  is how a deployment hands this manager its capability, and using a naming
  convention as a trust boundary is how `AuthorityPort`'s own operands were
  invisible.

2. A MEMBER SET IS NOT A FIELD CONTRACT

  Exact POD and an exact member set are the SAFE REPRESENTATION of a document.
  They say nothing about what its members MEAN, and three well-formed documents
  proved it: an integer `authority_uuid` was issued and put in an operation
  signature; a claim answer naming another participant was durably recorded; and
  a text `generation` reached SQLite's INTEGER column as a raw IntegrityError
  AFTER the authority had answered the claim -- the worst moment to find out,
  because the authority holds a live assignment and the manager has no record.

  The injected documents' members are now entries in the same lexical universe,
  discovered structurally: a member READ on a value whose origin is a capability
  answer, followed one level into this module's own helpers, and keyed to the
  CROSSING rather than to the reading site. `issue_offer` reading five members
  of a projection the port obtained is the port's crossing seen from further in,
  not five more crossings.

  Twelve new member entries, owned:

    project_work.authority_uuid       identity
    claim / committed result          the whole assignment document, then
      .work_ref                       document
      .work_ref.authority_uuid        identity
      .work_ref.work_id               identity AND compared to the Work asked
      .participant                    text AND compared to the session binding
      .generation                     a whole number counting from zero
    settle_operation.record           document (reason, disposition)
    settle_operation.detail           text when present
    settle_operation.result           the assignment, by the other path

  TWO OF THE FOUR PARTS ARE RELATIONSHIPS. A perfectly well-formed identity
  naming another participant or another Work is not this manager's assignment,
  and recording one would durably attribute somebody else's claim to this offer.
  Those are compared, not shaped -- which is why `settle_operation` now takes the
  Work the settlement is about.

  ONE OWNER FOR TWO PATHS. The claim answer and a committed settlement's result
  are the same document arriving twice, so `_assignment` owns both. Writing the
  rule at one of its two sites is this campaign's recurring defect, and the
  committed path is exactly where a late record goes to the same columns.

3. THE DUPLICATE REFUSAL ADOPTION

  `_operation_row` owned the journal row and proved its refusal column decodes;
  `replay` then called the public `revive_refusal`, which adopted the same bytes
  again. The reviewer patched the second adoption and made an exact durable
  replay fail, which is the proof it was reachable.

  Split rather than weakened, per 4bz's deletion half. The `json` column
  contract now names the members it must decode to, so the whole sealed pair is
  owned ONCE at the read; `_revived` is the constructor over an already-owned
  document, and the public `revive_refusal` keeps its own full boundary for a
  caller holding raw sealed text.

WHAT THE MACHINERY CAUGHT THIS ROUND

  Two things, before any reader did.

  A shared owner building its labels from the caller's noun produced a label
  with no literal part at all, and the inventory refused it on the spot -- a rule
  that is applied and cannot be SEEN to be applied is one the next reviewer takes
  on trust. Labels are now literal-or-template: the fragment is what the source
  says, and a probe asserts the FULL label, so a probe aimed at the claim answer
  cannot be satisfied by the committed claim's refusal.

  And driving the generation range turned up a redundancy rather than a missing
  case: the frozen bound is already owned by the exact-POD rule that owns the
  document the member arrives in. My range check was a second owner for one
  property, and unreachable besides. It is gone -- the third unreachable boundary
  this campaign has made me delete rather than document.
