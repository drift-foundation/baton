  Before: all of these returned satisfied=true with broad, extra and
  unaccounted all empty. The evaluator's answer is unchanged — they do
  authorize — and the audit now refuses every one of them.

== What changed, and why it is a REMOVAL rather than a decoder ==

stringLiteral treated every backslash sequence as syntax it understood:
it decoded \n and \t and otherwise dropped the backslash and copied the
next character. So \x64ocker read as x64ocker — a rule for nothing,
invisible to the Docker audit — while Starlark decodes it as docker.

The correction is round 1's lesson applied one level down: do not
reimplement the language. The ONLY escapes accepted are the three this
module's own generator can emit — backslash-backslash, backslash-quote
and backslash-apostrophe, which is what JSON.stringify produces for a
backslash or a quote inside an operand. Every other escape is refused, so
hex, unicode, long-unicode, octal and anything neither of us has thought
of all land in UNACCOUNTED and fail closed. Raw and triple-quoted strings
were already unaccounted and still are.

FAIL-CLOSED IS NOT FAIL-BLIND. A generated policy for a path containing a
backslash, or a participant containing a quote, still round-trips through
its own auditor — there is a regression for exactly that. A deployment
needing any OTHER escape cannot be audited by this preflight, and that
refusal is the honest answer: its exact evaluator semantics would have to
be established against the oracle first, not guessed a second time.
