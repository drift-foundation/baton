WHAT THIS SLICE IS, AND WHAT IT IS NOT

  PLAN 4be names attempt axes, activation, cancellation ordering, output freeze,
  intake and cleanup. This delivers the first three of those in the order the
  frozen host itself is sliced in:

    record_attempt        the attempt an accepted offer named, under an
                          operation identity that signs every durable operand
    activate_assignment   bind the attempt to THIS attempt's own committed
                          claim, through the participant-bound session, fixing
                          all four parts before anything writable runs
    observe               per-axis transitions, decided and written in one
                          savepoint against the exact value the update compares,
                          journalled by SOURCE IDENTITY

  ABSENT RATHER THAN STUBBED, and named in the module's own docstring: runtime
  start, reconciliation and cancellation ordering -- which introduce the injected
  runtime adapter and agent boundaries -- and then output freeze, intake and
  cleanup. `attempts.mjs` carries the same sentence for the same reason: what a
  conforming adapter must BE is a later item's to pin, and until then positive
  absence cannot be proven.

THE SCHEMA

  Version 5. `attempts` with the four assignment columns under an all-four-or-
  none CHECK, ten axis columns each constrained to its own vocabulary, and
  `observations` keyed by `(attempt, incarnation, source_seq)` with a second
  unique index on the manager's own ordering. A partial unique index makes one
  claimed offer per attempt.

  THE VOCABULARY AND THE TRANSITIONS ARE DIFFERENT THINGS. The CHECK constraints
  say what an axis may SAY; `TRANSITIONS` says what may FOLLOW what, frozen all
  the way down. Treating the vocabulary's order as a transition order is how
  `worker_disposition=completed` once advanced to `unable` -- a different
  terminal ANSWER, not a later stage of the same one. A case asserts the two
  agree, because a vocabulary written in two languages is two vocabularies that
  agree until they do not.

WHAT THE BOUNDARY MODEL DID TO THIS CUT

  The inventory is now load-bearing rather than descriptive, and writing a new
  module against it was the first real test of that. It caught three things
  before any reader:

  1. A SHARED OWNER WITH NO LITERAL IN ITS LABEL. `_optional(value, what)` took
     the caller's noun whole, and the inventory refused it on the spot -- the
     second time this campaign a shared owner has been written that way and the
     second time the machinery, not a review, said so.
  2. A BLANKET RENAME THAT ATE ITS OWN DEFINITION. Making `fixed_assignment`
     private with a search-and-replace turned `def _fixed_assignment` into
     `def __fixed_assignment`, exactly the defect I recorded three cuts ago as
     "a rename is a refactor and I did it as a search-and-replace". Caught by
     the probe run in seconds; recorded again because I evidently need it
     written down more than once.
  3. TWO UNREACHABLE BOUNDARIES, deleted rather than documented. A
     `boundaries.count_of` over `COALESCE(MAX(x), 0) + 1` cannot fail: the
     column is INTEGER in a STRICT table and the empty case is the COALESCE.
     That is the fourth and fifth unreachable owner this campaign has made me
     remove.

  The universe grew from 148 entries to 194 without a single hand-written
  addition to the discovery: the new module's parameters, capability calls, SQL
  reads and member reads were found by the same rules. What I had to write was
  owners and probes.

  Four began as zeros; two were missing cases of one shape -- a guard whose
  condition ONE PROCESS CANNOT REACH ON ITS OWN. H6 needs a store written before
  the unique index, and H12 needs the observation insert itself to fail. Both
  are now driven the way they actually happen: a side connection that drops the
  index, and a trigger some other build could have put on the table. A guard
  that can only be reasoned about is a guard nobody has checked.

  H14 IS A MEASURED EQUIVALENCE AND THE FINDING IS THE MEASUREMENT. I wrote the
  case expecting activation's compare-and-swap to refuse when a second manager
  activates first, and it does not: both derive the SAME operation identity from
  the same operands, so the journal replays the first committed answer before
  the second act runs. Effectively-once settles the race a layer above the swap,
  and the swap is therefore not drivable through the public surface.

  I KEPT IT, and I am flagging that as a judgement call rather than a proof.
  Unlike the five unreachable VALIDATIONS this campaign has made me delete, this
  is the write's own condition -- the thing that makes the UPDATE conditional at
  all -- and removing it would leave a durable write unguarded against a future
  caller reaching it under another identity. The test now records what the race
  actually produces, including that exactly one journal row exists.

  H18 is the familiar shape: the exemption's entry is owned, so relaxing the
  assertion that says so changes no verdict. The counting is proved separately
  by a fabricated unowned exemption.

AN INSTRUMENT FAILURE, RECORDED

  The first mutation run exceeded a ten-minute execution cap and was killed
  BETWEEN writing a mutant and restoring the original, leaving `attempts.py`
  mutated. The next run's baseline was consequently dirty, and every row in it
  reported the same failure the baseline already had -- a fourth instrument-
  failure shape after the false zero, the false non-zero and the stale bytecode.

  Two corrections: the restore moved into a `finally`, so an interrupted run
  cannot leave a mutant behind, and the run moved off the capped path. The
  discarded results are not reported. The tree was repaired by rewriting the
  original text, not by a Git operation.
