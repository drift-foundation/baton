1. ADOPTED ENTRIES NOW COME FROM THE SQL, NOT FROM THE VALIDATORS

  The universe of persistent reads is discovered by parsing the SQL each
  `execute` is handed and keeping every SELECT, keyed by the table it names.
  That structure exists whether or not anybody owned the result, which is the
  property the previous two versions lacked. Five crossings:

    offers.py:_offers                  offers        -> boundaries.row
    offers.py:_certified               meta          -> boundaries.text
    store.py:ControlStore._adopt       meta          -> boundaries.text x2
    store.py:ControlStore._operation_row  operations -> boundaries.row
    store.py:ControlStore._objects     sqlite_master -> STATED, see below

  `boundaries.row` is new and owns a row's COLUMN SET as well as its values,
  against a per-table contract that lives beside the DDL in schema.py. The
  contract and the CHECK constraint are the same statement in the two
  vocabularies each end can enforce: the CHECK binds what THIS build writes, and
  an adopted row is by definition one some other process wrote.

  THREE READ SITES BECAME ONE. The offers table was read from the lookup, the
  expiry sweep and restart recovery, and three sites is three chances to forget.
  A caller cannot now obtain an offer row without passing through `_offers`.

  `sqlite_master` is declared with a reason instead: SQLite's catalogue names
  objects with identifiers it accepted at CREATE time, and this read decides one
  membership question. I wrote a `boundaries.text` there first and could not
  drive it -- a boundary no caller can reach is precisely what my last round was
  corrected for, so it is absent rather than decorative.

  A new column kind, `json`, proves a persisted result or refusal DECODES at the
  read and hands back the bytes that were stored, because replay is byte-stable.
  `replay` therefore no longer decodes through the layer: the value crossed at
  the read, and decoding it a second time would be the blanket revalidation the
  same ruling forbids. A failure there is now a defect and faults as one, the
  same rule `deadline` follows with its already-owned instant.

2. CLOSED MEANS CLOSED, IN BOTH DIRECTIONS

  `document(value, what, required, optional)` refuses a member its contract does
  not name. An extra member has two readings and both are alarming -- a build
  this one was not written against, or a document that is not the one we think
  it is -- and ignoring it silently picks the happier one.

  `alternative` now takes a per-variant `(required, optional)` contract rather
  than a vocabulary. `{"kind": "committed"}` was a complete settlement, and the
  offer advanced to `claimed` carrying a null assignment -- the exact outcome the
  `committed` branch exists to record faithfully. The four variants are
  live/record, committed/result, retired/record, refused/detail, and the
  retirement's bound record is owned at the same crossing because the manager
  reads BOTH its members to choose the control state it writes.

  The projection contract is split into the five members the manager READS and
  the ten it knows the authority emits. Naming the unread ten is what lets the
  contract be closed without pretending the authority sends nothing else -- and
  the split is checked against the authority's own source, not against the fake.

3. THE INVENTORY, KEYED THE SAME WAY THREE TIMES

  An entry is `(domain, lexical site, subject)` where the site is module, class
  and function. The universe, the owner and the probe are looked up by that same
  tuple, so a repeated label cannot stand in for two entries and a probe cannot
  cover an entry it was not aimed at.

    58 receiving entries    46 caller, 7 injected, 5 adopted
    43 probes               one per (entry, label) the layer or a delegate owns
    28 stated owners        each naming a witness test that must exist
     5 delegated             the label is read from the DELEGATE's own code
     3 declared exceptions   boundary calls that are not receiving entries

  Two corrections fell out of building it. `replay`'s `kind` and `signature`
  were declared DELEGATED to `_agreeing` and are not: `_agreeing` is called by
  `transact`, and replay COMPARES both against the journalled row. And
  `accept_offer`'s `reason` does not reach SQL unowned -- it rides the
  settlement's manager signature, and canonicalization refuses it. Both were
  written down wrong by me and found by the machinery rather than by a reader.

4. THE OUTBOUND CONSTRUCTORS, WHICH WERE OWED

  4bz's third part, unimplemented for two rounds and named in my own handoff.
  `documents.py` holds one contract table and ten constructors; every operation
  answers through them.

  The reason it matters is not tidiness. `_settle_terminal` returns one document
  when its compare-and-swap wins and a different one when it loses, and
  `_record_claim` is reached from four callers each contributing its own
  members -- so the shape of an answer was a property of the PATH rather than of
  the operation, and a caller reading one return statement could not learn what
  the others answer with. A document whose members depend on which branch built
  it cannot be owned at the far end against anything.

  The constructors establish the SHAPE and nothing else. Owning our own outbound
  values would be the same blanket revalidation, one direction over.
