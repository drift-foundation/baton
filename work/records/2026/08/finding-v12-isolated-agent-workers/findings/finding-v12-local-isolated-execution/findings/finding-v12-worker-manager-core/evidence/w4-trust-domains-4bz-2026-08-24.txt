THE THREE DOMAINS, AND WHAT WAS MISSING

  boundaries.DOMAINS = ("caller", "adopted", "injected")

  caller    an operand a caller handed us.
  adopted   persistent data coming back out of our own store. Ours once; a
            receiver input again the moment it is read, because the process that
            wrote it is not this one and the bytes may not be either.
  injected  the answer of a capability trusted deployment supplied. Trusted to
            be the authority's; not trusted to be correct.

  Only `caller` existed before this round. The two that did not exist are
  exactly where the review's three P1s were, which is the finding: I had been
  patching sites inside the one domain I had named while two whole domains
  crossed the boundary unowned.

WHAT NOW OWNS WHAT

  injected -- every answer, at the port, not at its users:
    project_work      document(required=status, phase, handler, gate,
                               authority_uuid)
    slot_holder       text
    claim             document(required=work_ref, participant, generation)
    settle_operation  alternative(live|committed|retired|refused)
    claim_signature   injected
  adopted -- at the decode, which is where the domain is crossed:
    replay            adopted(row["result"])
    revive_refusal    document(adopted(sealed), required=category, code, message)

  Three kinds were added to carry these: `document` (an exact fresh dict,
  optionally with exactly these members), `alternative` (a document that must be
  ONE OF a closed set, discriminated), `adopted` (persistent text decoded and
  owned, refusing rather than faulting).

WHAT WAS DELETED, WHICH IS THE OTHER HALF OF THE RULING

  4bz forbids blanket revalidation as plainly as it requires ownership, and the
  double validations were mine:

    deadline()      re-owned its from-instant AND re-owned its own answer.
    accept_offer()  owned offer_id twice.
    issue_offer()   re-owned authority_uuid already owned in the projection.

  The from-instant case is the instructive one. Every caller passes an instant
  this layer has already proved, so the re-proof was unreachable -- and in the
  previous round I had written it up as an "unreachable entry with a reason",
  which sounded like rigour. It was not: it was me documenting a double
  validation as though it were a defended edge. A boundary no caller can drive
  is usually a boundary that should not be there. A defect that fails there now
  faults as the caller's ownership defect, which is what it is.
