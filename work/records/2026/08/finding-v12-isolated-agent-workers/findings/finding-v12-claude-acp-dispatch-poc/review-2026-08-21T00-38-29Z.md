## Findings

### P1 — an ambiguous recap still releases completed Work for another execution

`Manager.returnJob()` calls `baton.say()` directly at
`src/manager.mjs:783-786`. The operation now carries an id, but the manager
neither replays that id nor reconciles the message after a lost result. If the
message commits and its result is lost, `returnJob()` throws before `pass`, the
outer catch compensates the already-completed assignment, and the Job becomes
available for another execution. A later attempt uses a new runtime-attempt id
and publishes a second recap.

This does not satisfy round four's requested “effectively once or explicitly
non-load-bearing” behavior. Carrying an operation id makes a retry possible;
it does not perform one. Put `say` through an effectively-once path with a
canonical reconciliation rule, or move it after the authoritative handoff and
define/report its failure without undoing valid completed Work. Add a
commit-then-lose-result regression that proves the result is not re-executed
and the recap is not duplicated.

### P2 — successor claims make committed pass/release reconciliation look failed

The projection fallback for `pass` requires the review route **and** a null
Handler (`src/manager.mjs:799-804`); `release` likewise requires a null Handler
(`src/manager.mjs:269-279`). Those observations are transient. After either
mutation commits, the destination or original queue may be claimed before the
fallback detail read. The manager then sees a legitimate successor Handler,
declares its own committed mutation absent, and may report `stranded` or enter
the wrong compensation path.

Reconciliation must answer whether *this manager's claim* is still held, not
whether nobody has claimed since. For release, any Handler other than the
expected releasing participant proves that participant's claim is gone. For
pass, the ruled destination route plus a Handler other than the source
participant proves the handoff occurred; an immediate destination claim must
not turn success back into ambiguity. Add races where the next eligible member
claims before the projection fallback.
