## P1 — settlement timeout can hide an already committed claim

The ruled token boundary fixes one claim operation at durable acceptance, and
§8/§9 require ambiguous claim settlement through that exact operation.
`Manager.settlement_timeout`, however, checks only whether the control-store
offer still says `accepted`.

The failing trace is:

1. the offer is durably accepted and fixes `claim:<offer>`;
2. the authority commits that operation, mints generation 1, records Handler,
   and consumes the participant's deployment-wide claim slot;
3. the manager loses the result before changing the offer to `claimed` or
   recording the attempt;
4. settlement timeout sees only `accepted` and changes it to
   `settlement-expired`; and
5. after restart, `Manager.claim` refuses "offer was not accepted" instead of
   replaying the committed operation.

The authority now has a live assignment with no recoverable manager attempt,
exactly the ambiguity the contract exists to prevent. The timeout must settle
or query the fixed claim operation before terminalizing the offer. A committed
claim wins and must be recorded as `claimed`; only a claim proven not to have
committed may become `settlement-expired`.

Additive regression:
`test_settlement_timeout_cannot_hide_an_already_committed_claim`.

## P1 — effectively-once stops at the authority boundary

Several exact retries replay the authority result and then repeat or regress a
control-store mutation:

- Retrying `Manager.cancel` after the runtime was positively destroyed replays
  the authority cancellation, then blindly rewrites runtime state from
  `destroyed` back to `cancel-requested`. This loses the strongest quiescence
  observation and can prevent the gate from settling.
- A stale-target integration appends its journal attempt and raises before
  `_replay` records an operation result. Retrying the same integration op
  appends a second attempt; if the target later returned to the old value, the
  same operation identity could take a different outcome.
- `Manager.freeze` has no operation identity or immutability check. A second
  call replaces the digest of output already described as frozen and
  immutable.
- The replay signatures for `Authority.end`, `advance_contract`, and `close`
  omit their durable reason/rationale. Reusing one operation id with different
  prose returns the first successful result instead of refusing conflicting
  effective operands, even though that prose is written into the authoritative
  event or contract history.

These are not cosmetic model gaps: they contradict §7's “every authority
mutation is effectively-once,” §9's recovery table, and invariants 9 and 13.
Give manager-owned mutations stable operation records as well as authority
operations, preserve monotonic terminal observations on replay, make freeze
immutable, and include every effective durable operand in replay signatures.
A refused operation that journals an attempt must durably replay that refusal
without appending again.

Additive regressions:

- `test_exact_cancel_retry_does_not_regress_destroyed_runtime`
- `test_refused_integration_retry_journals_one_attempt`
- `test_frozen_output_cannot_be_rewritten`
- `test_assignment_end_replay_binds_the_durable_reason`
- `test_contract_transition_replay_binds_the_durable_rationale`
- `test_close_replay_binds_the_durable_rationale`

generation and the first v12 claim mints generation 1. The model and SPEC
implement that reading correctly. The durable decision history does not yet
make the supersession explicit:

- the parent “Assignment-generation identity” ruling still says every
  successful atomic claim generates an assignment-generation ID;
- this child FINDING repeats that unqualified statement under “Confirmed
  parent decisions”; and
- `SPEC.md` §1 says the contract supersedes no confirmed parent ruling while
  §5 narrows generation minting to v12 contracts.

Repository policy requires an explicit dated supersession when a later ruling
narrows an earlier live rule. Add the scoped clarification in the parent owning
record, mirror it in this child's decision summary, and make §1 acknowledge
that narrow supersession. The old reasoning stays; only its broad reading is
superseded.
