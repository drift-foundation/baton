## P1 — the separate settlement deadline is specified but not modeled

The approver ruling separates token expiry from a later claim-settlement
timeout. `SPEC.md` sections 6, 7, and 9 require an accepted and still
unsettled offer to be past a visible settlement deadline before its fixed
claim operation may be retired.

The model has no settlement deadline. `Offer` stores only the bearer-token
acceptance expiry, and `Manager.settlement_timeout` checks no clock or durable
deadline at all. It can therefore retire the fixed claim operation
immediately after the `issued -> accepted` transition, before the authorized
claim has any settlement window. The additive trace accepts at time 10 and
calls the timeout in that same instant; the offer becomes
`settlement-expired` and the fixed claim is dead.

Record a distinct durable claim-settlement deadline (or an equivalent
authority-visible policy boundary) and compare it before commit-or-retire.
It must not reuse the bearer acceptance expiry: ruling 2 makes those separate
boundaries. A pre-deadline timeout attempt leaves the offer `accepted` and the
fixed claim operation live.

## P1 — settlement accepts a committed result with the wrong operands

`Authority._replay` correctly binds a committed operation to its signature,
but `Authority.operation_result` and `settle_operation` inspect only
`operation_id`. The settlement act therefore treats any committed value under
that ID as this offer's fixed claim result without comparing the operation's
kind and participant binding.

The additive trace accepts an offer for `baton.codex`, then submits the same
operation ID as a claim for `baton.claude`. The later exact claim for Codex
correctly refuses because the ID was reused with different operands, but the
manager catches that refusal and terminalizes. Settlement sees the other
committed result and changes Codex's offer to `claimed`, binding Claude's
assignment to it.

Commit-or-retire must take and validate the fixed claim signature. A prior
committed or durable-refused record with different operands is an operation-ID
collision and must fail closed without changing either record. An unsubmitted
identity may be retired only while binding the expected fixed claim operands.
The desired later behavior — every submitter sees retirement before its own
operand mismatch — can still hold after a correctly bound retirement; it
cannot justify accepting or overwriting a pre-existing different operation.
