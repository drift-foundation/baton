Reviewer: `baton.claude`, under the "Second implementer design review
checkpoint". Work `W2`, claimed 2026-08-20. Critique only: no implementation,
prototype, dependency, schema or product-code change was made.

Method: the complete record re-read against the tree, including the changes
that landed *after* the first review — W2571 (pass requires the current
claim), W2645, W2693, W2780, W2597, W2938 (one-slot capacity and the

Findings are labelled **Confirmed** (measured in the tree), **Observed**,
**Inferred** or **Open**.

## The first review's ten findings are resolved

Recorded so the next reader does not re-open them. §1 by the dated V11
enforcement boundary; §2 by the assignment-generation identity ruling, which
correctly makes the successful claim the minting act and separates it from
`episode_seq`, incarnation and config generation; §3 by the authority-mediation
boundary, which resolves it better than I proposed — read-only describes the
worker's repository/execution capability, and the manager owns the authority
channel entirely, so the sandboxed model never has to cross it; §4 by the
four-gate verify/review/approve/integrate ruling; §5 by the workspace-aware
Git-mutation cutover, including the requirement that policy and enforcement
change together; §6 in part by the dossier-ownership ruling (see §E below for
what it leaves open); §7 by the conflict-handling ruling, which replaces the
model's judgement with a mechanical rule — conflict-free replay or stop; §8 by
the testable credential and quiescence boundary; §9 by probation-without-
rerouting, which is a better answer than mine — dynamic eligibility beside the
configuration rather than inside it, with a typed `no-certified-runtime` gate
so unavailable never reads as offered; and §10 by the manager-clock ruling.

Two of those answers are worth calling out as improvements on the review that
prompted them: the authority-mediation boundary, and `no-certified-runtime`
as a visible scheduler gate rather than a silent absence.

## New findings

### A. One-slot capacity is now an authority fact, and the record does not know it

**Confirmed.** `transitions.claim_work` refuses a second live claim by the same
participant (`src/baton_work/transitions.py`, "participant holds ONE active
claim at a time"). That landed under W2938 after this record's rulings were
pinned and is currently in the working tree under review.

The record says "Exactly one worker runtime may execute under a live assignment
generation" — a per-generation rule. It never says how many live generations
one PARTICIPANT may hold, and until today that was a v12 design choice. It no
longer is: the authority permits exactly one, because the generation is minted
by the claim and a participant may hold one claim.

Consequences the record should state rather than leave to be discovered:

- a participant cannot run two workers concurrently, so any scheduling that
  assumed per-participant parallelism is refused at the authority, not at the
  manager;
- "Parallel child Work remains safe because each child owns a distinct claim,
  proposal, and dossier" is true only across DIFFERENT participants now;
- and the manager's "offer to another certified configured handler" becomes
  the only parallelism available on a shared route.

**Recommendation.** State the capacity unit in the record, cite where it is
enforced, and say explicitly whether v12 intends to keep one-slot capacity or
to widen it. Widening it would be a protocol change with its own supersession,
not a worker-manager decision — and W2938's own reasoning is that an undefined
capacity unit makes "free capacity" unknowable.

### B. Two overlapping "nobody claimed" mechanisms, with two authoritative clocks

**Confirmed, and this is the finding I would resolve first.**

W2938 also added a canonical PARTICIPANT PICKUP OBLIGATION: an idle
participant whose actionable pool is non-empty owes exactly one pickup; the
interval start is stored in the authority; `pending` versus `overdue` is
derived at READ time from the authority's clock against an accepted policy
value; and it surfaces as Teams `Pickup`, a bold row, and `[Teams *]`.

V12's claim-acceptance deadline measures an overlapping condition — an offered
participant that has not claimed — but with a different owner (the Worker
Manager), a different clock (its monotonic clock, explicitly authoritative for
deadline detection), a different threshold, a different scope (one assignment
attempt rather than one participant), and different consequences (typed
contradiction plus route policy, versus a visible obligation).

Both can be true and disagree: a participant can be `overdue` on the
authority's pickup clock while its worker attempt is inside the manager's
claim-acceptance deadline, or the reverse. An operator reading Teams and an
operator reading worker status would get different answers to "is anybody
failing to pick this up".

**Recommendation.** Rule which one owns the question. The cleanest reading is
that the authority's pickup obligation is the OPERATOR-facing fact (it is
canonical, restart-surviving, and already presented) while the manager's
deadline is an internal attempt-lifecycle bound that must not be surfaced as a
second, competing overdue state. If both are to be visible they need distinct
names and a stated relationship, because "overdue" already means something in
the product.

Related and narrower: the record says the manager's clock is authoritative for
deadline detection and that worker/provider/engine timestamps are diagnostic —
but it never mentions the AUTHORITY's clock, which stamps `claimed_at`,
`handoff_at` and the pickup interval and derives pickup state. There are now
two authoritative clocks for adjacent facts. That is probably fine, and it
should be said rather than left implicit.

### C. `plan-rejected` can loop

**Observed.** A worker that rejects a plan "stops execution, returns a typed
`plan-rejected` disposition ... and ends its claim and assignment generation",
and "plan rejection does not automatically close the Work". A revision then
requires a new claim and generation.

Nothing in the record prevents the Work from being re-offered to the same
profile with the SAME unrevised plan the moment the claim ends. Under the
current authority it will be: ending the claim makes the participant idle, the
Work is still open, ready and routed, so it re-enters the actionable pool and
the pickup obligation restarts immediately. A well-behaved worker rejects it
again, and the loop is bounded only by the unreliable-profile counters — which
would then blame the profile for correctly refusing a bad plan.

**Recommendation.** Make plan rejection change the Work's schedulability, not
only the claim: park or gate it on the plan-revision decision, so the next
offer cannot precede the revision. Note that under W2571 a parked or blocked
Work can no longer be passed either, so the gating and the reissue path need
stating together.

### D. Plan immutability contradicts how plans are actually maintained

**Confirmed against practice.** "The worker ... does not rewrite its own plan
during implementation", with parent status "derived from Baton authority or
updated separately by the parent's owner".

But a leaf record's own `PLAN.md` carries per-item status marks
(`[queued]`/`[in progress]`/`[done]`), and today the implementer maintains
them — every Work in this session did. The record assigns parent plans to the
parent's owner and progress to `PROGRESS.md`, and leaves the leaf plan's own
status lines unowned.

**Recommendation.** Choose one and say it: either the leaf plan's status marks
move to `PROGRESS.md`/authority-derived state, or "immutable" is scoped to the
plan's CONTENT with status marks explicitly exempt. This is small, but it is
exactly the kind of gap that surfaces as a merge conflict on every proposal.

### E. An isolated worker cannot file a discovery

**Observed, and it contradicts standing repository policy.** `AGENTS.md`:
"Discovery is recursive and role-neutral. Either role may immediately file a
new defect; filing does not interrupt the serial queue." That is not
incidental — this session alone filed W2645 and W2693 from inside other Work,
and the policy on Baton defects makes filing mandatory before any workaround.

Under the pinned v12 model a worker updates only its own record's
`PROGRESS.md` and Work-scoped evidence; parent plans and `work/open/` belong
to trusted coordination tooling; and a new finding needs a new record
directory, a `work/open/` symlink, and a bound ledger Work. None of those is
available to a worker inside its private clone.

**Recommendation.** Give the worker-control API a `file-finding` intent the
manager mediates — the same shape the authority-mediation boundary already
uses for claim, pass and close. Without it, either the policy silently stops
applying to workers or workers will smuggle findings into their own
`PROGRESS.md`, which is where nobody schedules them.

### F. Minor: the compact generation form must never be accepted as input

**Confirmed.** The record proposes rendering a generation as `W2@g3`. v11's
Work-selector resolver is strict — canonical `<authority>-W<seq>` or exactly
`W<positive-int>`, with everything else refused by name (`test_w4_selectors`).
`W2@g3` would refuse, which is correct.

**Recommendation.** State that the compact form is display-only and never a
selector, so no client round-trips it into a Work-valued operand.

## Does the bounded spike answer the remaining questions?

**Partly, and the gap is worth naming before ordering.**

What it covers well: the happy path end to end, plus the two hardest safety
properties — cancelling a live worker and proving a returning stale generation
cannot publish. Those are the right things to test-drive first, and seeding the
conformance suite from its traces is the right deliverable.

What it does not exercise, all of it newly pinned and therefore least settled:

- **plan rejection** (§C) — including whether the Work is re-offered;
- **the conflict stop** — a target that moves under a proposal with a real
  content conflict, which is the path that must NOT be resolved by the model;
- **the claim-acceptance deadline and its route-policy consequence** — the
  record explicitly moved this from "always cancel" to "policy decides", and
  the spike is where a policy clause first meets a real timer;
- **the four gates as four** — the spike lists review and approval, but a
  walking skeleton where one participant performs both would not notice if the
  implementation collapsed them.

What it cannot answer at all, and should say so: credential profiles and
scanning, retention policy, cache sharing without writable cross-worker state,
signing/attestation, and the proposal-store layout. Those remain on the open
list after the spike, and ordering should not assume otherwise.

**Recommendation.** Add plan-rejection, a real conflict stop, and one
claim-deadline policy clause to the spike's scenario list; make review and
approval two participants in the skeleton; and state which open decisions the
spike is NOT expected to close.

## Disposition

No blocking objection. The record is markedly stronger than at the first
review, and the authority-mediation and probation rulings resolve their
findings better than the review that raised them.

§A and §B are the two I would settle before ordering, because both come from
protocol facts that landed after these rulings were pinned and both change
what the manager may assume. §C, §D and §E are each small to rule and each
becomes expensive if discovered during implementation. §F is a one-line note.

W2 returns to `baton.feat`. No implementation is authorized by this review and
none was performed.
