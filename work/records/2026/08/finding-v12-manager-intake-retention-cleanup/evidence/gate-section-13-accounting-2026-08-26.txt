W6629 — evidence for PLAN item 7e, the section 13 accounting for the new
public surface.

Written under the same claim, after the full suite corrected the earlier
verification section. What was outstanding: `test_secrets` requires every
exported callable to be classified into one of its two closed sets, and every
surface classified as CONSTRUCTING to be proved by a probe that drives the real
exported operation with the bearer live. Ten exported names had no accounting
and three durable writers had no coverage, and two gates were red because of
it.


THE TWO RED GATES, AND WHAT THEY WERE SAYING

    tests.manager.test_secrets.EveryDurableWriterIsGuarded
      .test_every_durable_writer_is_covered
        ('intake.py', '_retain', 'retentions')
        ('intake.py', '_seal', 'intake_artifacts')
        ('intake.py', '_seal', 'intakes')

    tests.manager.test_secrets.EveryPublicSurfaceIsAccountedFor
      .test_every_exported_callable_is_in_exactly_one_class
        authorize_cleanup   collect_operation   decide_retention
        destroy_operation   intake_operation    intake_receipt_of
        record_intake       request_intake      retain_operation
        retentions_of

  Both universes are DERIVED -- the writers from the AST, the surfaces from
  `__all__` -- so neither list is one somebody remembered. That is why they
  found this at all.


THE DURABLE HALF: THREE WRITERS, ONE REASON EACH

  Both writers are private and each has exactly one door. `record_intake`
  reaches `_seal`; `decide_retention` reaches `_retain`; both go through
  `store.transact`. The journal row is written INSIDE that transaction and
  before the COMMIT, and it carries the full effective signature and the
  byte-stable result -- so a bearer in either is refused with the action's own
  writes still inside the transaction that takes them back.

  The reason each entry gives names the path that actually runs, which is what
  the two interrogation entries above them in the same table were corrected
  for twice. `_seal`'s `why` is the one column here that is COMPOSED rather
  than adopted -- it is built from the live assignment the port answered with
  -- and it is a member of the receipt the journal walks.


THE PUBLIC HALF: SIX PROBED HERE, FOUR PROBED WHERE THEY CAN BE REACHED

  CONSTRUCTS_A_PORTABLE_ARTEFACT, each with a probe in `test_secrets`:

    collect_operation     the four derived identities. Each is handed the
    intake_operation      caller's ATTEMPT MAPPING and answers with an
    retain_operation      operation id and a signature digest composed from
    destroy_operation     it, so `manager_signature`'s own reason applies
                          unchanged: the identity is PORTABLE and a guard at
                          the eventual write runs after the caller holds it.

    intake_receipt_of     the two read-side doors, for the reason
    retentions_of         `certified_agent_session_profile` was added for: a
                          write-side guard cannot see a later store edit, and
                          one of these hands back the digest a destroy is
                          AUTHORIZED by.

  RETURNS_NO_CONSTRUCTED_ARTEFACT, with the walk named and the drive
  elsewhere:

    request_intake        each composes text it did not own and each walks it
    record_intake         before it answers -- but each refuses for a missing
    decide_retention      attempt long before reaching that walk, so a probe
    authorize_cleanup     against `test_secrets`' fixture would pass for the
                          wrong reason and report a guard it never ran.

  This is `record_frozen_result`'s situation exactly, and it is resolved the
  way that entry resolves it: the reason names the walk, the walk is probed
  directly, and the door itself is driven where a fixture can reach it --
  `tests.manager.test_intake.EveryJournalledDoorRefusesALiveBearer`, one case
  per door plus one control.


THE PROBES ARE FACTS, MEASURED BY REMOVING THE GUARDS

  Six probes, source mutated and restored byte for byte each time.

    walks removed                     probes that stopped refusing
    -------------------------------   ----------------------------------
    boundaries.row's walk only        none
    intake.py's five walks only       collect_operation, intake_operation,
                                      retain_operation, destroy_operation
    both                              all six

  So the four derived identities are guarded by intake.py's own walk and by
  nothing else -- there is no row crossing in them to fall back on -- and the
  two read-side doors are guarded TWICE, at the row crossing and again over
  the assembled document. Removing either one alone still refuses; it takes
  both. That is what makes the read-side entries defence in depth rather than
  one of them being dead code, which is the question the measurement was run
  to answer.

  THE FOUR JOURNALLED DOORS ARE LAYERED THREE DEEP, and the same method
  says so:

    intake.py's walks muted           all four still refuse
    ... and manager_signature too     all four still refuse (the journal)

  Each case proves its door refuses -- which is what §13 asks -- and not
  which of the three did it. The named owner is the FIRST; the ones behind it
  are why removing one of them is not a leak. Recorded in the test class
  rather than only here, because the next reader meets the test.

  AND A REFUSAL IS NOT EVIDENCE OF A WALK. Every one of these doors refuses a
  missing attempt, an unfrozen output and an undecided artifact too, so a
  case that never got past those would look exactly like a passing §13 case.
  `test_the_walk_is_what_refuses_and_not_a_precondition` drives the same
  operands with the bearer FORGOTTEN and requires each door to get through.


ONE THING THE GATES DID NOT CATCH

  `schema.__all__` did not declare `CUSTODY`, `RETENTION_DISPOSITIONS`,
  `INTAKE_COLUMNS`, `INTAKE_ARTIFACT_COLUMNS` or `RETENTION_COLUMNS`, while
  every other table's column contract and closed vocabulary in that module is
  declared. Nothing failed: the package reads them as module attributes and
  the text sweep drives them from the package surface rather than from that
  list.

  That is the observation worth keeping rather than the fix. This module has
  no gate comparing what it DEFINES to what it DECLARES, so the omission was
  invisible to a suite that is otherwise built out of derived universes. The
  names are declared now; the missing gate is not this Work's to add and is
  left for review to place.


THE BOUNDARY INVENTORY, RE-ATTRIBUTED

  The earlier progress entry said "after 7b and 7c none of my entries is among
  them" about the boundary-inventory failures. That was wrong, and re-reading
  the gates one at a time is what showed it.

  `test_every_stated_owner_names_a_witness_that_exists` was ENTIRELY W6629's.
  Ten stated owners had no witness and every one of them was this Work's:

    documents.py:collect_requested   documents.py:intake_artifact
    documents.py:intake_receipt      documents.py:retention
    documents.py:retention_decided   documents.py:cleanup_blocked
    documents.py:cleanup_unsettled   documents.py:cleanup_settled
    intake.py:decide_retention  artifact_ids
    intake.py:request_intake    adapter.collect

  A stated owner is a CLAIM until something exercises it, which is what that
  gate is for. Eight are outbound constructors and their witness is derived
  from `documents.CONTRACTS` rather than listed, so it already exercised them
  and only the entry was missing. The other two state a rule of their own and
  are witnessed by two new cases: an adapter that reports success and answers
  with something that is not a collection, and a retention decision whose
  artifact list is wrong in each of the three ways it can be wrong. The gate is
  green.


THE ORPHAN GATE IS NOT THIS WORK'S, AND THE MEASUREMENT IS WHY

  `test_every_boundary_call_belongs_to_an_entry_or_is_declared` reports 18
  boundary calls the inventory cannot attribute to any entry. Six are
  `intake.py`'s. The other twelve are four other modules':

    intake.py:_compared             5   W6629
    intake.py:decide_retention      1   W6629
    oci.py (six sites)              7   W6632 / W6633
    posture_slots.py (three sites)  3   W6627
    interrogation.py:_diagnostics   1   W6627
    workspaces.py:_advertised       1   W6631

  EVERY ONE OF THE EIGHTEEN HAS THE SAME SHAPE: a boundary call whose subject
  is a caller-origin local inside a private helper, or a comprehension
  variable. `_calls_in` propagates a helper's calls to its caller's site only
  when the subject resolves to `session:` or `read:`, so a caller-origin rule
  written in a shared owner is invisible from the crossing that reaches it.
  This is one gap in the resolver, met independently by five modules.

  AND THE TWO DECLARED ESCAPES CANNOT TAKE IT. `DELEGATED` is the mechanism
  for an owner that lives at another site, and adding one here was MEASURED
  rather than assumed:

    layer_labels(('caller','intake.py:record_intake','collected.result_id'))
      -> ['a collection observation']
    claims with DELEGATED added -> 2, and `test_no_entry_is_owned_twice`
      requires <= 1

  The member is already claimed by the COVERING owner -- `boundaries.document`
  over the whole collection -- so declaring the per-member owner as well is
  double ownership, which is the rule 4bz put there. `NOT_AN_ENTRY` is the
  other escape and it says "this subject is not a receiving entry"; these
  subjects ARE receiving entries, so writing them there would be a false
  statement to make a gate green.

  So this Work does not resolve it. The condition is recorded, attributed and
  left for review to place: the correction belongs to the resolver or to a
  ruling about what a covering owner claims, and either is a decision above
  one module's slice.
