W6629 -- evidence for the correction review's [P1], and for an honest gate.


THE [P1], AND IT WAS TWO OF MY OWN RULES CONTRADICTING EACH OTHER

  `_retain` deliberately stores ONE CURRENT DECISION PER ARTIFACT, and its
  conflict update lets a later policy replace one artifact without touching its
  peers. `retentions_of`, as I wrote it in the previous round, grouped the rows
  that are current NOW by their operation id, derived a command from each
  group, and required that derived artifact set to equal the historical
  committed one.

  Those cannot both hold. One valid command decides A and B; a later valid
  command replaces B alone; A still names the authentic A+B act while the
  current group for that act contains only A. The reader derived an A-only
  operation, found the journal carrying the A+B one, and reported a forgery --
  blocking retention reads and cleanup over a row nobody had touched.

  I introduced the grouping in the previous round to make the reconstruction
  match a command that decides a SET. That was the right instinct about the
  COMMAND and the wrong question to ask of a ROW.


THE CORRECTION

  Per surviving row. For each row, load the committed `output.retain` result
  the row's own stored operation id names, and require that result to include
  THIS artifact under THIS disposition and THIS policy digest. A historical
  peer that has since been re-decided is irrelevant to whether this row is
  authentic, and a deleted decision leaves its artifact undecided for cleanup
  rather than making a different, still-authentic row invalid.

  The forged-row property is unchanged and is what the same comparison
  enforces: an edit to `disposition` or `retention_policy_digest` makes the row
  disagree with the committed act it names.

  runner status. That is correct and it is worth stating plainly rather than
  explaining away: I had been reading verdicts off the unittest summary line
  after piping through `grep` or `tail`, which discards the runner's status,
  and my backgrounded runs ended in `echo`, so the exit codes reported back to
  me were the WRAPPER's status and not the gate's.

  practice was not evidence, and a green wrapper around a red gate is exactly
