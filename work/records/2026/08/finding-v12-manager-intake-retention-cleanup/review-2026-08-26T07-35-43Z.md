## Findings

### [P1] Refuse cleanup while the fixed assignment is still live

`authorize_cleanup` (`intake.py:736-825`) verifies the participant but never
asks the authority whether the exact fixed assignment remains live. It then
calls the destructive adapter. The predecessor W4 decision is explicit:
destruction waits until the assignment is ended or fenced, because otherwise
the manager tears out a worker the authority still authorizes to execute.

Correction boundary: read the live assignment through the authority port and
refuse a genuinely new destroy while it equals the attempt's fixed assignment.
Keep exact committed replay above today's liveness check. Prove that no adapter
call occurs while live and that cleanup succeeds after end/fence.

Regression: `IndependentContractReview.
test_cleanup_waits_until_the_assignment_is_ended_or_fenced`.

### [P1] Deliver the complete retain and destroy commands to the adapter

`decide_retention` (`intake.py:608-672`) validates `adapter.retain` but never
calls it. `_destroyed` (`intake.py:868-913`) calls `adapter.destroy` with only
`runtime_id`. Thus one frozen command is omitted and the other loses the
assignment, attempt, receipt digest, policy digest and operation identity that
authorize it. W4's prototype already passed the destroy assignment, attempt,
runtime and operation across this boundary; the frozen schema now defines the
complete bodies.

Correction boundary: deliver `outputRetainBody` plus its operation to
`adapter.retain`, and `runtimeDestroyBody` plus its operation to
`adapter.destroy`. Do not infer authority inside the adapter. Preserve the
current rule that only the adapter's positive observation of the exact runtime
settles absence.

Regressions:

- `test_retention_is_delivered_to_the_adapter_as_a_command`;
- `test_destroy_is_delivered_with_the_authorizing_contract_body`.

### [P1] Distinguish retention commands made under one policy

`retain_operation` (`intake.py:205-237`) includes only attempt identity and
policy digest. `outputRetainBody` also requires `artifact_ids` and
`disposition`; those operands distinguish valid acts. With two artifacts, a
policy decision retaining the first and discarding the second produces one
operation id and two signatures, so the second command is rejected as an
operation collision.

Correction boundary: include the canonical artifact set and disposition in
the derived operation identity (and keep the complete body in its signature),
while preserving exact retry replay and the ability for a later policy to
replace a decision.

Regression: `test_one_policy_can_decide_different_artifact_groups`.

### [P1] Authenticate cleanup authorization rows with committed journal evidence

`intake_receipt_of` (`intake.py:536-603`) verifies only that the reconstructed
receipt hashes to `intakes.receipt_digest`. An edit that changes both the row
and that adjacent digest is accepted. `retentions_of` (`intake.py:700-734`)
does not compare reconstructed decisions with the journal at all; a direct
edit of disposition and policy becomes valid destroy authorization.

This is the exact durability class already closed and signed off in W4:
loaded intake decisions are authenticated by the committed operation
signature, because a foreign key or self-consistent row proves no committed
decision identity. Retention rows now participate in the same cleanup
authorization and require the same fail-closed binding.

Correction boundary: canonically reconstruct each stored decision's operation
signature from the row and its immutable attempt/assignment context, require
matching committed journal evidence, and report divergence as integrity rather
than a caller collision. Keep read-side secret walks after reconstruction.

Regressions:

- `test_a_self_consistent_receipt_edit_is_not_journal_evidence`;
- `test_a_retention_row_edit_cannot_authorize_cleanup`.
