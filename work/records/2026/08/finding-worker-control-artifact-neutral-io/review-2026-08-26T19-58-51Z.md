## Finding

### [P1] A completed receipt still need not bind its completion envelope

Control SPEC §7.3 requires the manager to validate `/output/output.json`, hold
it against the exact input manifest, and only then produce its frozen-result
receipt. But §8.4 says `completion_manifest_digest` is optional, and
`resultManifest.required` omits it. The shipped regression
`test_a_receipt_may_still_omit_the_binding_today` confirms that a 1.0 receipt
with `disposition: completed` and no completion digest is accepted.

That is not a rollout boundary: the version schema admits only 1.0, no
capability negotiates the legacy path, and omission carries no durable marker
distinguishing “no worker envelope existed” from “the manager declined to bind
the envelope it validated.” The contract therefore still gives two normative
answers about the same completed result.

Use the measured first correction: under 1.0, a `completed` result receipt MUST
carry `completion_manifest_digest`. Non-completed dispositions may omit it
when the worker died before publishing; whenever any completion envelope was
validated, the manager binds its digest regardless of disposition. Do not
widen the version vocabulary merely to preserve the bypass.

The reported 96 refusals are migration cost in fixtures that construct the
changed frozen contract. Their Work provenance does not make those fixtures a
compatibility promise, and it does not justify leaving the 1.0 shape
permanently ambiguous. Update the shared/helpers or individual fixtures, keep
the focused contract gate green, and retain a negative case proving a
completed receipt without the digest is refused.

Two nearby comments must move with that correction: the test class currently
claims minor 1 is the stable boundary even though the schema pins minor to 0,
and `_check_completion_manifest` says W6634 “performs” the cross-document
comparison although W6634 has not implemented envelope intake yet. State the
landed rule and the downstream obligation, not an unreachable version or a
future implementation as present fact.
