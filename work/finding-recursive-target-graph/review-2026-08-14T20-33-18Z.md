## R1 — participation is still treated as a contribution ACL

**Blocking authority/UX defect.** The approved matrix says every configured
participant may contribute an ordinary message, add `+` attention, and change
their own seen state; those operations grant no workflow ownership.
`post_message` instead refuses unless the author's team is already present in
`work_participants`, and `detail.available_transitions` hides both
`post_message` and `mark_seen` from a configured member who drills into linked
Work. There is no way for that curious reader to chip in without somebody
first including their team, which restores the barrier the open-graph ruling
explicitly removed.

Permit an authenticated configured member to post ordinary/`+` discussion on
open Work without prior Work participation. Record the author's team as a
participant as part of the committing transaction so subsequent New/accounting
has a durable basis. Keep every owner-only transition gated exactly as it is;
the regression also proves that contributing still does not permit close.
Expose `post_message` and own `mark_seen` accordingly.

## R2 — `available_transitions` does not mirror live writer preconditions

**Blocking machine-interface defect.** The projection is both over- and
under-restrictive:

- it hides `close` whenever an explicit blocker is open, although the ruled
  writer deliberately permits an honest terminal rejection/duplicate/defer
  while a provider is still open; only open children prevent closure; and
- it offers `reopen` for a closed child beneath a closed parent, although the
  writer refuses until the ancestry is reopened.

Agents must not discover either fact by attempting the operation. Derive the
projection from the same child-only close gate and parent-open reopen gate as
the writer. The old projection assertion that a blocker suppresses `close` is
stale and contradicts both the authority and the recorded ruling; replace it
with the additive regression's expectation rather than changing writer
semantics.

## R3 — one return object is not one SQLite snapshot

**Blocking consistency defect.** `home` fetches rows and then calls
`team_summary` through separate autocommit SELECTs. A writer can commit between
them, producing an old `phase=queued` row beside a new `parked=1` summary. The
new parity test only compares sequential calls after all writes have stopped;
it does not exercise the promised snapshot boundary.

The WAL-mode reviewer regression commits a phase change exactly between the
two reads and observes the torn result. Hold the complete home projection in
one explicit read snapshot (and ensure the envelope's `snapshot_seq` describes
that same snapshot rather than a later commit). Preserve read purity and do
not turn projection reads into receipt writes.
