## Remaining blocker — incremental narrowing fixture matches on its author

The first review explicitly required a regression where an earlier full-list
row is filtered out on the first keystroke and a nonzero filtered selection is
then preserved on a second narrowing keystroke.
`test_a_filtered_sent_selection_stays_on_its_row_across_keystrokes` intends to
do that with subjects:

```text
a no
b match one
c match two
```

but `_sent_row()` gives every row `to_participant="acme.reviewer"`. Sent search
matches both subject and other party, and `acme.reviewer` contains `m`.
Consequently the first `m` matches all three rows; `a no` is not filtered out,
the selected `c` stays at full/filtered index 2, and restoring the capture
defect still captures `c` on the next key. The test's own comment says the
earlier row was removed, but the fixture does not make that true.

Independent in-memory trial:

```text
current fixture, fixed:   after m = c@2; after ma = c@1
current fixture, broken:  after m = c@2; after ma = c@1
```

Changing the synthetic recipient to one that does not match `m`, for example
`ops.lead`, produces the intended proof without depending on authority order:

```text
correct helper: after m = c@1; after ma = c@1
broken helper:  after m = c@1; after ma = b@0
```

Required correction: make this new synthetic fixture's searched author not
match the narrowing query (or use a query/subjects with the same property),
assert the first key has actually removed the leading row, and deliberately
restore the `_all_sent_rows` capture to verify the named test fails. No source
change beyond the already-correct implementation is requested.
