## R1 — the real `m` key still refuses every newly authorized no-claim row

Severity: **blocking functional defect**.

`InboxState.materialize_selected_part` implements the widened rule, but it is
not the dispatch boundary. `driver._allowed` gates `K.MATERIALIZE` through
`InboxState.affordances()` before the method can run. The still-live affordance
at `src/baton_tui/state.py:848` requires `claim`, and its refusal at
`src/baton_tui/state.py:904` still says materialization needs a held claim.
Consequently answered/closed messages, Sent rows, and seen notices all fail at
the driver even though the method below would authorize them.

Measured through `driver.step`, not by calling the model method:

    answered row affordance: False
    press m: materialize needs a message you hold the claim for; this row has none
    projection files: []

The new tests miss this because the answered, sent, and seen-notice cases call
`state.materialize_selected_part(...)` directly
(`tests/tui/test_tui_materialize_boundary.py:103`, `:120`, `:145`). They prove
the lower method but bypass the product key path that refused Slawomir.

Required correction:

1. Make the `materialize` affordance express the ruled condition: selected
   non-external part, projection directory, and content already readable. It
   must be true for a live claim, answered/closed inbound, Sent, and seen
   notice; false for unclaimed inbound-pending.
2. Make `unavailable_reason("materialize")` agree with that same predicate and
   retain the helpful “Enter opens it” explanation for unclaimed pending mail.
3. Add driver-level `step(..., ord("m"), ...)` regressions for at least the
   answered row and seen notice, plus the negative unclaimed-pending case. A
   direct state-method test is not sufficient for this boundary.
4. Update the stale `materialize_selected_part` docstring, which still says the
   operation is from an “EXPLICITLY OPENED active claim.”
